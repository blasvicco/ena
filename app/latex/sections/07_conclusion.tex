\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a small set of skills can turn the solutions an evolving population produces into a memory that mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and introduced the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) to answer it. On the tested sequences, the answer is yes. The memory works only when it is organized by the physics the agent detects, and recalling from it by Bayesian policy reuse works better than following the detector alone. It is built online, without labels, by a detector suited to each domain, and the search that fills it is worth scaling to failure. Single-policy learners trained on the same sequences show how much is lost without such a memory, and also its price: ENA adapts more slowly to a new environment and, on HalfCheetah, reaches lower returns.

The most direct next step is a hybrid ENA, in which gradient-based learners such as SAC or PPO act as fast optimizers within a niche, to adapt sooner and reach higher returns, while ENA's environment detection, Plasticity and physics-indexed Hall of Fame protect what each niche has learned across environment switches. Such a hybrid is also the right agent to compare against continual-RL systems designed for the same problem (OWL~\cite{kessler2022same}, RL-CD~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}, MBCD~\cite{alegre2021minimum}, SWOKS~\cite{dick2024statistical}, TeLAPA~\cite{lillo2026beyond}). The skills may also carry over to those methods: OWL and TeLAPA rely on being told where tasks begin and end, and their authors name detecting task boundaries during training~\cite{kessler2022same} and weaker task boundaries~\cite{lillo2026beyond} as future work, while ITE starts adapting at a user-set performance threshold and keeps no memory across conditions~\cite{cully2015robots}. ENA's environment detection, which supplies both the changes and the identity of a returning environment from the agent's own observations, could give each of them what it is now told or lacks, and its physics-indexed archive could keep their adapted solutions for when a condition returns. Several directions follow from the limitations. Repeated recurrence during training, randomized task orders and many more environments would test the memory beyond three phases. Nonlinear dynamics models, still fitted without gradients, would separate physics that a linear model merges. For systems that cannot be reset, spectral identification could superimpose low-amplitude dither on operational actions, or use posture-independent probing reflexes. The contributions of the belief's two sources of evidence, the dynamics likelihoods and the test returns, remain to be separated, and specialist selection could be learned as a contextual bandit over the detector's evidence. The evolution strategies that Nisioti et al.~\cite{nisioti2026continual} found to balance adaptation and forgetting well could replace ENA's simple search operators, with environment detection and the Hall of Fame on top. Finally, the population's evaluations are independent and parallelize naturally on accelerators, which would allow larger populations and longer non-stationary streams.
