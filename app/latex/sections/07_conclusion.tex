\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a small set of skills can turn the solutions an evolving population produces into a memory that mitigates catastrophic forgetting, and introduced the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) to answer it. On the tested CartPole sequences, the answer is yes: among the agent's variants that learn the environments, only the one whose archive is organized by the physics it detects keeps them, and recalling from that archive by Bayesian policy reuse works better than following the detector alone. The memory is built online, without labels, by a detector suited to each domain. Failure-driven search is what fills it, at the price of more interaction; whether scaling search to failure beats spreading the same effort evenly remains open. Single-policy learners trained on the same sequences show how much is lost without such a memory, and also its price: on CartPole ENA adapts more slowly than PPO, and on HalfCheetah it reaches lower returns than SAC.

The most direct next step is a hybrid of ENA with gradient-based learners, which can take several forms. In one, learners such as SAC or PPO act as fast optimizers within a niche, to adapt sooner and reach higher returns, while ENA's environment detection, Plasticity and physics-indexed Hall of Fame protect what each niche has learned across environment switches. In another, gradient updates refine archived specialists before they are deployed, or the population supplies diverse experience to a gradient learner, as in evolutionary reinforcement learning~\cite{khadka2018evolutionary}. A hybrid is also the right agent to compare against continual-RL systems designed for the same problem (OWL~\cite{kessler2022same}, RL-CD~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}, MBCD~\cite{alegre2021minimum}, SWOKS~\cite{dick2024statistical}, TeLAPA~\cite{lillo2026beyond}).

The skills may also carry over to those methods. OWL and TeLAPA rely on being told where tasks begin and end, and their authors name detecting task boundaries during training~\cite{kessler2022same} and weaker task boundaries~\cite{lillo2026beyond} as future work, while ITE starts adapting at a user-set performance threshold and keeps no memory across conditions~\cite{cully2015robots}. ENA's environment detection, which supplies both the changes and the identity of a returning environment from the agent's own observations, could give each of them what it is now told or lacks, and its physics-indexed archive could keep their adapted solutions for when a condition returns.

Several directions follow from the limitations. A skill ablation on HalfCheetah, a control that spends the same search effort evenly instead of by failure, and repeated recurrence during training, randomized task orders and many more environments would test the memory beyond the evidence here. Nonlinear dynamics models, still fitted without gradients, could separate physics that the detector now merges. For systems that cannot be reset, the probe could be superimposed on the agent's own actions as a small dither. The contributions of the belief's two sources of evidence, the dynamics likelihoods and the test returns, remain to be separated, and specialist selection could be learned as a contextual bandit over the detector's evidence. Finally, the population's evaluations are independent and parallelize naturally on accelerators, which would allow larger populations and longer non-stationary streams.
