\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) never computes a gradient. It maintains a population of $N$ small neural-network policies, the \textit{specialists}, each carrying a scalar \textit{Trust} score. In every training episode one specialist acts, its return updates its Trust, a \textit{Plasticity} value decides how much of the population is re-evaluated, the strongest specialists may be archived in a \textit{Hall of Fame}, and the population evolves. On top of this loop, two skills make the archive usable in a non-stationary stream: \textit{environment detection} notices when the physics change and recognizes physics it has seen before, and \textit{specialist selection} chooses which archived specialist to deploy. Each mechanism is implemented as an interchangeable module with an ``off'' setting, which is what allows the ablation in Section~\ref{sec:results}. Fig.~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarizes one training episode.

\subsection{Inspiration}
\label{sec:inspiration}
ENA borrows two strategies from nature, each through a mechanism already established for evolutionary algorithms in changing environments.

\subsubsection{Variation in proportion to stress (Plasticity)}
Bacteria under stress increase their mutation rate. This is a regulated and transient response: variation is raised when the current genotype is failing, and kept low otherwise, because in a well-adapted organism most mutations are harmful~\cite{galhardo2007mutation}. Evolutionary computation adopted the same idea as \textit{triggered hypermutation}, raising the mutation rate when performance drops in a non-stationary environment~\cite{cobb1990investigation}, and more generally as the control of an algorithm's parameters during the run~\cite{eiben1999parameter}. ENA's Plasticity extends this response from the mutation rate to the whole search effort. The failure signal~\eqref{eq:ramp} sets how many specialists are re-evaluated (a random subset of size $k$), how long each is evaluated ($s$ steps), and how far mutants move from their parents. The share of the population replaced in each generation stays constant. What scales with failure is the amount of evidence gathered before selection and the size of the steps taken after it. The reason is cost: every candidate step is an environment step, so a converged agent should spend almost none, while an agent that has just met new physics must search broadly. In addition, freshly initialized specialists enter the population in every generation, as \textit{random immigrants}~\cite{grefenstette1992genetic} do, keeping enough diversity to follow a moving optimum.

\subsubsection{Consolidated memory (Hall of Fame)}
The complementary learning systems theory~\cite{mcclelland1995there} holds that the brain learns quickly in the hippocampus and consolidates slowly into the neocortex, where knowledge is protected from interference by new learning. The theory was proposed in part to explain why the brain does not suffer the catastrophic interference of gradient-trained networks~\cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic}. ENA follows the same division of labor. The population is the fast learner, overwritten every generation; the Hall of Fame is the consolidated store, entered only through a gate, never modified, and never replaced by evolution. In evolutionary computation, a \textit{hall of fame} first kept the best individuals of past generations in competitive coevolution~\cite{rosin1997new}, and memory-enhanced evolutionary algorithms store good solutions and reinsert them when earlier conditions return~\cite{branke1999memory}. ENA adds two things to this kind of memory: it is indexed by the physics in which each specialist succeeded (Section~\ref{sec:detection}), and recall is a Bayesian decision rather than a reinsertion (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).

\subsection{Population and Specialists}
Each specialist is a multi-layer perceptron with orthogonal initialization. Its inputs are normalized with a running mean and variance. Because those statistics keep drifting while the agent explores, a specialist's first layer is analytically re-scaled to the current statistics before it acts, so its behavior is preserved without any retraining. During training the acting specialist is chosen $\epsilon$-greedily: with probability $\epsilon$ a random non-archived specialist, otherwise the one with the highest Trust.

\subsection{Trust and Plasticity}
Both quantities derive from the same failure signal. Let $H$ be the history of the last \Res{cfg/ena/history} episode returns, $P_{q}(H)$ its $q$-th percentile and $R_{\min}$ the worst return seen so far. For a return $R$,
\begin{equation}
f(R) =
\begin{cases}
1 & |H| < \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} \\
\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min} & R \ge P_{\Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}}(H) \\
1 - \Res{cfg/ena/signal/slope}\,\rho(R) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ramp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\rho(R) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{R - R_{\min}}{P_{\Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}}(H) - R_{\min}},\, 0,\, 1\right).
\end{equation}
A return at or above the \Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}th percentile gives the minimum signal $\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min}$; the signal grows linearly to $1$ at the worst return observed. We call $f$ the \textit{linear ramp}.

\subsubsection{Trust}
The acting specialist's Trust is an exponential average whose rate is the failure signal $D=f(R)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T_{t+1} &= (1 - D)\,T_t + D\,\hat{r}(R), \\
\hat{r}(R) &= \frac{R - P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/plow}}(H)}{P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/phigh}}(H) - P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/plow}}(H)},
\end{split}
\label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{r}$ clipped to $[\Res{cfg/ena/trust/low}, \Res{cfg/ena/trust/high}]$, deliberately not to $[0,1]$, so a specialist that beats the current record still ranks above the rest. A poor episode moves Trust quickly; a good one only nudges it, which punishes failure fast and rewards success slowly. When $D > \Res{cfg/ena/trust/reset}$, the Trust of every non-archived specialist is also blended toward a reference value (the normalized mean of $H$) by the factor $D$, which re-opens the competition after a failure.

\subsubsection{Plasticity}
Plasticity $\mathcal{P} \in [0, 1]$ sets the search effort of the episode, and a \textit{controller} sets it from the episode's return (Section~\ref{sec:controllers}). It selects $k$ of the other $N-1$ specialists as candidates and evaluates each for at most $s$ steps,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
k &= \min\left(N-1,\, \max\left(\Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates}, \lfloor N\mathcal{P} \rfloor\right)\right), \\
s &= s_{\min} + (s_{\max} - s_{\min})\,\mathcal{P},
\end{split}
\label{eq:plasticity}
\end{equation}
in a copy of the current environment (a truncated return is rescaled to a full-episode equivalent). Their Trust is updated with~\eqref{eq:trust}, and $\mathcal{P}$ also scales the mutation noise (Section~\ref{sec:search}). A confident agent therefore evaluates \Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates} candidates briefly and mutates gently, while an agent that is failing searches broadly and deeply. Every candidate step is a real environment step and counts against the agent's budget.

\subsubsection{Plasticity controllers}
\label{sec:controllers}
A Plasticity controller maps the return of an episode to $\mathcal{P}$. Trust always uses the linear ramp~\eqref{eq:ramp} as its rate; the Plasticity controller is configured separately. We use five:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Linear ramp}, ENA's controller: $\mathcal{P} = f(R)$~\eqref{eq:ramp}.
\item \textit{Zero}: $\mathcal{P} = 0$ in every episode. This is the minimum search: $k=\Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates}$ candidates for $s_{\min}$ steps each, and mutation noise at its floor. The ablation uses it to switch Plasticity off.
\item \textit{Full}: $\mathcal{P} = 1$ in every episode. Every other specialist is evaluated for $s_{\max}$ steps, and mutants receive the largest noise. It serves as the reference for a fixed maximum effort (Section~\ref{sec:compute}).
\item \textit{Quadratic}: $\mathcal{P} = \mathrm{clip}\big(((R_{\max} - R)/(R_{\max} - R_{\min}))^2,\, \Res{cfg/ena/signal/min},\, 1\big)$, where $R_{\max}$ is the best return seen so far. It stays low for moderate failures and rises steeply only toward the worst return.
\item \textit{Fuzzy}: a Mamdani controller~\cite{mamdani1975experiment} with two inputs. The first is the normalized return $\rho(R)$. The second is its recent trend: the mean of the last \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendrecent} returns minus the mean of up to \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendbefore} returns before them, divided by the same span, and $0$ until \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendmin} returns have been seen. Its \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/rules} rules raise Plasticity when the return is low or falling and lower it when the return is high and steady or rising. Inference is min--max with centroid defuzzification.
\end{itemize}
Like the ramp, the quadratic and fuzzy controllers return $1$ until \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} returns have been seen, and stay within $[\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min}, 1]$. They are the alternatives compared in Section~\ref{sec:controller}. At test time $\mathcal{P}=0$, whatever the controller. When the environment-detection skill confirms a change of physics, $H$ is cleared, so the ramp, quadratic and fuzzy controllers search at full effort until they have seen \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} returns of the new regime.

\subsection{Evolutionary Search}
\label{sec:search}
After every training episode, specialists are ranked by Trust, ties going to the older specialist; a specialist's age counts the episodes it has played, and a mutant inherits its parent's. The top \Res{cfg/ena/search/elite}\% form the elite pool. The bottom \Res{cfg/ena/search/replaced}\%, excluding archived specialists, are replaced. Of the replaced specialists, \Res{cfg/ena/search/mutants}\% become mutants of a random elite, \Res{cfg/ena/search/crossovers}\% become crossovers of two elites, and the remaining \Res{cfg/ena/search/fresh}\% are freshly initialized, which keeps the population diverse. With no archived specialist among them, each generation therefore replaces \Res{cfg/ena/search/mutantspop}\%, \Res{cfg/ena/search/crossoverspop}\% and \Res{cfg/ena/search/freshpop}\% of the population with mutants, crossovers and fresh specialists. A mutant perturbs each weight of its parent with probability $p_m$ by Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\Res{cfg/ena/mutation/minnoise} + (\sigma_m - \Res{cfg/ena/mutation/minnoise})\,\mathcal{P}$. A crossover takes each weight from either parent with probability $\Res{cfg/ena/crossover/share}$, after both parents are re-scaled to the current input statistics. New specialists start at the reference Trust.

\subsection{Hall of Fame}
The Hall of Fame stores frozen copies of proven specialists, the \textit{gladiators}, at most $K$ per \textit{niche}, a niche being a set of physics recognized by the environment-detection skill (Section~\ref{sec:detection}). Gladiators are never replaced by the evolutionary step. After each training episode, the acting specialist is considered for induction:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Gate.} Its Trust must stand out from the population, $z = (T - \mu_T)/\max(\sigma_T, \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatefloor}) \ge \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatez}\,(1 - \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gaterelief}\,\mathcal{P})$, or reach $T \ge \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatetrust}$. High Plasticity lowers the bar while the agent is still searching.
\item \textit{Niche.} The environment-detection skill resolves which niche the episode was played in.
\item \textit{Cloning, not relabelling.} A gladiator that also succeeds in a different niche is cloned into that niche, labelled with the current environment; the original keeps its home niche.
\item \textit{Diversity.} A candidate whose weights have cosine similarity above $\Res{cfg/ena/hof/similarity}$ with a gladiator of the same niche only replaces that twin if it has higher Trust.
\item \textit{Capacity.} A candidate fills a free slot of its niche, or replaces the niche's weakest gladiator if it is better.
\end{enumerate}
This design separates what must be kept from what must keep changing.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Retention.} A gladiator is a frozen copy, and the Trust stored with it at induction does not decay. It can be displaced only by a candidate of its own niche with strictly higher Trust, so the specialists of one environment can never be evicted by those of another, however long their environment is absent. Gladiators in the population are also exempt from the evolutionary replacement and from the Trust reset that follows a failure.
\item \textit{Plasticity.} Within a niche the archive keeps improving: any better candidate takes a free slot or the place of the weakest gladiator, high Plasticity lowers the gate, and success in a new niche creates a clone there instead of moving the original.
\item \textit{Olympic re-evaluation.} In domains where returns vary widely with initial conditions (such as continuous-control HalfCheetah), admission based on a single episode risks retaining specialists that were merely lucky. There ENA uses an \textit{Olympic} Hall of Fame driver: periodically (every 200,000 steps in HalfCheetah), all gladiators of the current niche compete in an ``Olympiad'' across 3 full episodes on shared seeds. Gladiators that fall below the population reference Trust are evicted, and surviving gladiators are confirmed with their multi-episode Olympic average return. At test time, only Olympic-confirmed gladiators are deployed.
\end{itemize}
Before testing, gladiators are re-injected into the population round-robin across niches, up to a share of \Res{cfg/ena/hof/reinject} of the population.

\subsection{Environment Detection}
\label{sec:detection}
Following the context-detection idea of da Silva et al.~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}, each niche $j$ owns a model of the environment's dynamics, and a niche is recognized by how well its model predicts the transitions the agent actually experiences. Our models are linear-Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1} - s_t = W_j^{\top}[s_t, a_t, 1] + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_j^2)\right),
\label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
fitted in closed form by ridge least squares from accumulated sufficient statistics, so the detector is gradient-free as well. Conditioning on the action makes the evidence independent of which specialist is playing, and every real transition of every episode is evidence.

\subsubsection{Detection}
The standardized residuals of the current niche's model, with the variance widened by a tolerance $\tau^2$ ($\tau = \Res{cfg/ena/detect/tau}$) to absorb the linear model's own error on nonlinear physics, are monitored with the Predictive-Mixture CuSum (PM-CuSum) of Halme, Poor and Koivunen~\cite{halme2026sequential}, a recent successor of Page's CUSUM~\cite{page1954continuous}. With $q = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ the pre-change distribution of the residuals $z_t$,
\begin{equation}
S_t = \max(S_{t-1}, 0) + \log \frac{\hat{p}_t(z_t)}{q(z_t)},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{p}_t$ is a Fixed-Share~\cite{herbster1998tracking} mixture of Gaussian predictors fitted on sliding windows of $2, 4, \dots$ recent residuals. An alarm is raised when $S_t > \log\gamma$, thresholded in accordance with the theoretical average run length to false alarm of at least $\gamma$ ($\gamma = 10^{\Res{cfg/ena/detect/gammaexp}}$ steps) derived by Halme, Poor and Koivunen~\cite{halme2026sequential} for exact pre-change distributions. In our empirical setting, where the pre-change distribution is an online-fitted linear-Gaussian approximation on nonlinear dynamics, $\log\gamma$ acts as a calibrated decision threshold whose false-alarm behavior is evaluated empirically (Section~\ref{sec:results} and Table~\ref{tab:detection}). We depart from the Gaussian example of~\cite{halme2026sequential} in one respect: each window also estimates a variance, and that variance is one-sided (never below the pre-change one), because a wrong dynamics model changes the spread of the residuals at least as much as their mean, and can only fit worse, never better.

\subsubsection{Identification}
The recent transitions (up to \Res{cfg/ena/detect/recent}) are recognized as niche $j^{*}$, the niche of highest mean per-step log-likelihood, if $j^{*}$ explains them within a margin of $m = \Res{cfg/ena/detect/margin}$ nats per step of a model fitted on those same transitions. Otherwise the physics are new and a niche is created. Only niches fitted on at least \Res{cfg/ena/detect/minsamples} transitions take part.

\subsubsection{Detect, then verify}
An alarm is only a candidate. After \Res{cfg/ena/detect/verify} post-alarm transitions, the identification test is run against the alarmed niche. If that niche still explains them, the alarm reflected states its model had not yet covered: the transitions are added to the model and the CuSum restarts. Otherwise a change of physics is declared, and the post-alarm transitions seed the new regime's evidence. During the test the dynamics models are never updated: the detector still detects and identifies, but learns nothing new.

\subsubsection{Late identity binding}
A niche is assigned only when a specialist is considered for the Hall of Fame (step~2 above), from the transitions of the episode it just played. The resolved niche's model is refined with them, or a new niche is fitted on them.

\subsubsection{Chirp detection (HalfCheetah)}
\label{sec:chirp}
On HalfCheetah the transition models misfire. Their evidence is the acting specialist's own transitions, and in a contact-rich body every new behavior of a learning population (another gait, a flip, standing still) reaches contact states a niche's model has not seen, which read as new physics. On the detection benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:detection_benchmark}, averaged across \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} confirmation seeds, they confirm \Res{detbench/transition/changes} changes and create \Res{detbench/transition/niches} niches on average for three environments (where each individual run produces integer counts), and even with a single fixed gait played throughout they still confirm \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/changes} changes and create \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/niches} niches on average: the agent's own transitions are poor evidence of the physics.

ENA therefore plays a chirp, as in system identification with a known input signal~\cite{ljung1999system}. At every episode end it resets the environment and plays the same \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps}-step action sequence, whatever any specialist would do, and compares the observations it produces across niches. The excitation is multiresolution, a wavelet train: the \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/excitationwavelet} wavelet played at dyadic scales, from a segment of half the chirp down to the finest, so that slow and fast responses of the body are both excited. The response, the observations minus the chirp's starting observation, is decomposed per observation channel into a \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/featurewavelet} \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/transform} tree of depth \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/level}~\cite{coifman1992entropy}, dyadic and non-overlapping like the discrete wavelet transform~\cite{mallat1989theory} but splitting every band, which gives \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/bands} frequency bands of equal width; one log-energy is kept per band. Wavelet features of a system's response to a known excitation are the standard signature of a change of physics in structural damage detection and machine fault diagnosis~\cite{kijewski2003wavelet,peng2004application}. Probing an environment to infer its dynamics has been learned in reinforcement learning~\cite{zhou2019environment}; ENA's chirp is fixed, so its response cannot depend on the agent. The transforms are computed with PyWavelets~\cite{lee2019pywavelets}.

Each niche $j$ keeps the mean $\mu_j$ of its $n_j$ chirps' feature vectors; the per-feature variance $\sigma^2$ is pooled over niches, as it comes from the environment's reset noise. The mean $\bar{f}$ of the last $w = \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/window}$ chirps is tested against the current niche with
\begin{equation}
T_j = \Bigg(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d} \frac{(\bar{f}_k - \mu_{j,k})^2}{\sigma_k^2\,(1/w + 1/n_j)}\Bigg)^{1/2},
\label{eq:chirp}
\end{equation}
which is about 1 under the same physics ($d$ features). Above the margin $\Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/margin}$ a change is confirmed, and the most recent chirps identify the new physics: the known niche of lowest $T_j$ within the margin, otherwise a new niche (in training only). A niche is judged once it holds \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/minchirps} chirps. At test, each chirp's Gaussian log-likelihood under each niche is the dynamics evidence $L_j$ of the selection~\eqref{eq:bpr}. The chirp costs \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps} steps per episode (\Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/share}\,\% of a HalfCheetah episode), counted in ENA's step budget, and the environment's random stream is restored after it, so that episode starts are unchanged. CartPole keeps the transition models, which detect its changes (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_detection}).

\subsection{Specialist Selection}
\label{sec:selection}
At test time the weights are frozen and $\mathcal{P}=0$; the only adaptation left is the choice of which gladiator acts. We adopt Bayesian Policy Reuse (BPR)~\cite{rosman2016bayesian} as a principled probabilistic mechanism to fuse continuous dynamics evidence $L_j$ with observed scalar utility $u$, with the niches as types and every gladiator as a policy. The agent keeps a belief $\beta$ over niches and plays the gladiator of highest expected utility $\sum_{j}\beta(j)\,\mu_{j\pi}$, where $u = R/R_{\text{solved}}$ is the return relative to the one at which the environment counts as solved (so solving reads as $u = 1$), and $\mu_{j\pi}$ is the mean utility of policy $\pi$ in niche $j$. After each episode,
\begin{equation}
\beta'(j) \propto \beta(j)\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(u;\, \mu_{j\pi}, \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/noise}^2\right)\; e^{L_j},
\label{eq:bpr}
\end{equation}
where $L_j$ is the log-likelihood of the episode's transitions under niche $j$'s dynamics model~\eqref{eq:dynamics}: the dynamics evidence and the return evidence are combined in one posterior. Two changes adapt BPR to our setting.
\begin{itemize}
\item BPR builds its performance model offline, by playing every policy in every type. Ours is learned online: a prior of utility $\Res{cfg/ena/bpr/ownprior}$ for a gladiator in its own niche and $\Res{cfg/ena/bpr/crossprior}$ elsewhere, worth \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/prior} observation, updated with every observed utility weighted by the posterior.
\item BPR assumes one fixed type per trial. To follow a changing environment, a Fixed-Share step~\cite{herbster1998tracking} mixes back a uniform share: $\beta \leftarrow \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/mix}\,\beta' + \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/switch}/|\mathcal{J}|$. A confirmed change of physics also resets the belief to uniform.
\end{itemize}
While simpler heuristic selection policies (such as greedily deploying the top-performing specialist of $\arg\max_j L_j$) or learned contextual bandits are viable alternatives, BPR operates out-of-the-box with our likelihood and utility formulation under uncertainty; exploring alternative retrieval drivers is left as future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).
