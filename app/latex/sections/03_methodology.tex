\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Setting}
\label{sec:problem}
The agent meets a sequence of environments $\mathcal{M}_1, \dots, \mathcal{M}_K$. Each is a Markov decision process $\mathcal{M}_k = (\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P_k, r)$. All of them share the state space, the action space and the reward function. They differ only in their transition dynamics $P_k$, that is, in their physics. During training, the environments are presented one after the other, each for a fixed budget. In the skill ablation, the budget is a number of episodes; when ENA is compared with other learners, it is a number of environment steps (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The agent observes only states, actions, rewards and episode ends. It is never told when the environment changes, which environment it is in, or how many there are (Appendix~\ref{app:information}), and it keeps all of its state across the changes. After training, the agent is tested, frozen, on a sequence that contains every training environment and some it has never seen. Again, it is not told where one environment ends and the next begins.

An agent \textit{forgets} environment $\mathcal{M}_k$ to the extent that its test return there falls below its return at the end of $\mathcal{M}_k$'s training phase. Section~\ref{sec:metrics} measures this as retention and absolute forgetting. An agent \textit{adapts} when it learns each new environment within its budget. The goal is to adapt to every new environment without forgetting the earlier ones. This setting is narrower than continual RL in general~\cite{khetarpal2022towards}: the observation space, the action space and the reward stay the same, and only the physics change.

\subsection{Overview and Terms}
The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) never computes a gradient. It maintains a population of $N$ small neural-network policies, the \textit{specialists}, and each specialist carries a scalar \textit{Trust} score. In every training episode, one specialist acts, and its return updates its Trust. A \textit{Plasticity} value then decides how much of the population is re-evaluated. The acting specialist may be archived in a \textit{Hall of Fame}, and the population evolves. Two more skills make the archive usable when the environment changes. \textit{Environment detection} notices when the physics change and recognizes physics seen before. \textit{Specialist selection} chooses which archived specialist to deploy. Each skill is an interchangeable module with an ``off'' setting, which makes the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:results} possible. Fig.~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarizes one training episode.

The biological names are labels, not claims. In operational terms:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \textit{specialist} is one policy network of the population;
\item a \textit{gladiator} is a frozen copy of a specialist, stored in the Hall of Fame;
\item a \textit{niche} is a set of physics that the detector treats as one environment, numbered by ENA itself and never given by the experiment;
\item \textit{Trust} is a running score of a specialist's recent normalized returns, used to rank specialists;
\item \textit{Plasticity} is a number in $[0,1]$ that sets how much search effort an episode receives.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inspiration}
\label{sec:inspiration}
ENA borrows two strategies from nature. Each one works through a mechanism that evolutionary algorithms for changing environments already use.

\subsubsection{Variation in proportion to stress (Plasticity)}
Bacteria under stress increase their mutation rate. The response is regulated and transient. Variation rises when the current genotype is failing and stays low otherwise, because most mutations harm a well-adapted organism~\cite{galhardo2007mutation}. Evolutionary computation adopted the idea as \textit{triggered hypermutation}, which raises the mutation rate when performance drops in a non-stationary environment~\cite{cobb1990investigation}. More generally, it adopted the idea of controlling an algorithm's parameters during the run~\cite{eiben1999parameter}.

ENA's Plasticity extends this response from the mutation rate to the whole search effort. The failure signal~\eqref{eq:ramp} sets three things: how many specialists are re-evaluated (a random subset of size $k$), how long each is evaluated ($s$ steps), and how far mutants move from their parents. The share of the population replaced in each generation stays constant. What scales with failure is the evidence gathered before selection, and the size of the steps taken after it. The rationale is cost. Every candidate step is an environment step, so a converged agent should spend few of them, while an agent that has just met new physics needs to search more broadly. Freshly initialized specialists also enter the population in every generation, as \textit{random immigrants}~\cite{grefenstette1992genetic} do. Their purpose is to keep the population diverse enough to follow a moving optimum.

\subsubsection{Consolidated memory (Hall of Fame)}
The complementary learning systems theory~\cite{mcclelland1995there} holds that the brain learns quickly in the hippocampus and consolidates slowly into the neocortex. There, knowledge is protected from interference by new learning. The theory was proposed in part to explain why the brain does not suffer the catastrophic interference of gradient-trained networks~\cite{mccloskey1989catastrophic}. ENA follows the same division of labor. The population is the fast learner, overwritten in every generation. The Hall of Fame is the consolidated store. It is entered only through a gate, it is never modified, and evolution never replaces it.

In evolutionary computation, a \textit{hall of fame} first kept the best individuals of past generations in competitive coevolution~\cite{rosin1997new}. Memory-enhanced evolutionary algorithms store good solutions and reinsert them when earlier conditions return~\cite{branke1999memory}. ENA adds two things to this kind of memory. The memory is indexed by the physics in which each specialist succeeded (Section~\ref{sec:detection}). Recall is a Bayesian decision rather than a reinsertion (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).

\subsection{Population and Specialists}
Each specialist is a small multi-layer perceptron. On CartPole, its hidden layers have \Res{cfg/ena/brain} units, with orthogonal initialization. On HalfCheetah, they have \Res{cfg/cheetahena/brain} units, with normc initialization and a tanh output, as in the evolution-strategies policy of Salimans et al.~\cite{salimans2017evolution}. A specialist's inputs are normalized with a running mean and variance. These statistics keep drifting while the agent explores. Before a specialist acts, its first layer is therefore re-scaled analytically to the current statistics, so that its behavior is preserved without retraining. During training, the acting specialist is chosen $\epsilon$-greedily. With probability $\epsilon$, it is a random non-archived specialist; otherwise, it is the specialist with the highest Trust.

\subsection{Trust and Plasticity}
\label{sec:trust}
Trust and Plasticity both derive from the same failure signal. Let $H$ be the history of the last \Res{cfg/ena/history} episode returns, and $P_{q}(H)$ its $q$-th percentile. Let $R_{\min}$ be the worst return of the current regime. Both $H$ and $R_{\min}$ are cleared when a change of physics is confirmed (Section~\ref{sec:detection}). For a return $R$,
\begin{equation}
f(R) =
\begin{cases}
1 & |H| < \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} \\
\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min} & R \ge P_{\Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}}(H) \\
1 - \Res{cfg/ena/signal/slope}\,\rho(R) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:ramp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\rho(R) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{R - R_{\min}}{P_{\Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}}(H) - R_{\min}},\, 0,\, 1\right).
\end{equation}
A return at or above the \Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}th percentile gives the minimum signal, $\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min}$. Below it, the signal grows linearly, up to $1$ at the worst return observed. We call $f$ the \textit{linear ramp}.

\subsubsection{Trust}
The acting specialist's Trust is an exponential average, whose rate is the failure signal $D=f(R)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
T_{t+1} &= (1 - D)\,T_t + D\,\hat{r}(R), \\
\hat{r}(R) &= \frac{R - P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/plow}}(H)}{P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/phigh}}(H) - P_{\Res{cfg/ena/trust/plow}}(H)},
\end{split}
\label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{r}$ clipped to $[\Res{cfg/ena/trust/low}, \Res{cfg/ena/trust/high}]$. The clip is deliberately wider than $[0,1]$, so that a specialist that beats the current record still ranks above the rest. A poor episode moves Trust quickly, and a good one only nudges it. Failure is thus punished fast, and success rewarded slowly. Most episodes also re-open the competition. Whenever $D > \Res{cfg/ena/trust/reset}$, that is, whenever the return falls below the \Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}th percentile of $H$, the Trust of every non-archived specialist is blended by the factor $D$ toward a reference value, the normalized mean of $H$.

\subsubsection{Plasticity}
Plasticity $\mathcal{P} \in [0, 1]$ sets the search effort of an episode, and a \textit{controller} sets it from the episode's return (Section~\ref{sec:controllers}). Plasticity chooses $k$ of the other $N-1$ specialists as candidates, and evaluates each one for at most $s$ steps:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
k &= \min\left(N-1,\, \max\left(\Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates}, \lfloor N\mathcal{P} \rfloor\right)\right), \\
s &= s_{\min} + (s_{\max} - s_{\min})\,\mathcal{P}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:plasticity}
\end{equation}
Here $s_{\min} = \Res{cfg/ena/smin}$, and $s_{\max}$ is the full CartPole episode (\Res{cfg/ena/smax} steps) or \Res{cfg/cheetah/maxeval} steps on HalfCheetah. Candidates play in a copy of the current environment, and a truncated return is rescaled to a full-episode equivalent. Their Trust is then updated with~\eqref{eq:trust}. Plasticity also scales the mutation noise (Section~\ref{sec:search}). A confident agent therefore evaluates \Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates} candidates briefly and mutates gently, while a failing agent searches broadly and deeply. Every candidate step is a real environment step. When ENA is compared with other learners, these steps count against ENA's budget. In the skill ablation, whose budget is a number of episodes, they are extra interaction.

\subsubsection{Plasticity controllers}
\label{sec:controllers}
A Plasticity controller maps the return of an episode to $\mathcal{P}$. Trust always uses the linear ramp~\eqref{eq:ramp} as its rate, so only the Plasticity controller changes. We use five controllers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Linear ramp}, ENA's controller: $\mathcal{P} = f(R)$~\eqref{eq:ramp}.
\item \textit{Zero}: $\mathcal{P} = 0$ in every episode. This is the minimum search: $k=\Res{cfg/ena/search/mincandidates}$ candidates for $s_{\min}$ steps each, and the lowest mutation noise. The ablation uses it to switch Plasticity off.
\item \textit{Full}: $\mathcal{P} = 1$ in every episode. Every other specialist is evaluated for $s_{\max}$ steps, and mutants receive the largest noise. It is the reference for a fixed maximum effort (Section~\ref{sec:compute}).
\item \textit{Quadratic}:
\[
\mathcal{P} = \mathrm{clip}\left(\left(\frac{R_{\max} - R}{R_{\max} - R_{\min}}\right)^{2},\, \Res{cfg/ena/signal/min},\, 1\right),
\]
where $R_{\max}$ is the best return of the current regime. It stays low for moderate failures, and rises steeply only near the worst return.
\item \textit{Fuzzy}: a Mamdani controller~\cite{mamdani1975experiment} with two inputs. The first input is the normalized return $\rho(R)$. The second is the return's recent trend: the mean of the last \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendrecent} returns, minus the mean of up to \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendbefore} returns before them. The trend is divided by $P_{\Res{cfg/ena/signal/percentile}}(H) - R_{\min}$, the span of $\rho$, and clipped to $[-1, 1]$. It is $0$ until \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/trendmin} returns have been seen. The controller's \Res{cfg/ena/fuzzy/rules} rules raise Plasticity when the return is low or falling, and lower it when the return is high and steady or rising. Inference is min--max, with centroid defuzzification.
\end{itemize}
Like the ramp, the quadratic and fuzzy controllers return $1$ until \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} returns have been seen, and they stay within $[\Res{cfg/ena/signal/min}, 1]$. Section~\ref{sec:controller} compares them. At test, $\mathcal{P}=0$ whatever the controller. When environment detection confirms a change of physics, $H$ is cleared. The ramp, quadratic and fuzzy controllers then search at full effort until they have seen \Res{cfg/ena/signal/warmup} returns of the new regime.

\subsection{Evolutionary Search}
\label{sec:search}
After every training episode, the specialists are ranked by Trust, and ties go to the older specialist. A specialist's age counts the episodes it has played, and a mutant inherits its parent's age. The top \Res{cfg/ena/search/elite}\% form the elite pool. The bottom \Res{cfg/ena/search/replaced}\%, except archived specialists, are replaced. Of the replaced specialists, \Res{cfg/ena/search/mutants}\% become mutants of a random elite and \Res{cfg/ena/search/crossovers}\% become crossovers of two elites. The remaining \Res{cfg/ena/search/fresh}\% are freshly initialized, which keeps the population diverse. With no archived specialist among them, each generation thus replaces \Res{cfg/ena/search/mutantspop}\%, \Res{cfg/ena/search/crossoverspop}\% and \Res{cfg/ena/search/freshpop}\% of the population with mutants, crossovers and fresh specialists.

A mutant perturbs each weight of its parent with probability $p_m$, by Gaussian noise of standard deviation $\Res{cfg/ena/mutation/minnoise} + (\sigma_m - \Res{cfg/ena/mutation/minnoise})\,\mathcal{P}$. A crossover takes each weight from either parent with probability $\Res{cfg/ena/crossover/share}$, after both parents are re-scaled to the current input statistics. New specialists start at the reference Trust.

\subsection{Hall of Fame}
The Hall of Fame stores frozen copies of proven specialists, the \textit{gladiators}. It holds at most $K$ gladiators per \textit{niche}, a niche being a set of physics recognized by environment detection (Section~\ref{sec:detection}). The evolutionary step never replaces a gladiator. After each training episode, the acting specialist is considered for induction, in five steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Gate.} Its Trust must stand out from the population, $z = (T - \mu_T)/\max(\sigma_T, \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatefloor}) \ge \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatez}\,(1 - \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gaterelief}\,\mathcal{P})$, or reach $T \ge \Res{cfg/ena/hof/gatetrust}$. High Plasticity lowers the bar while the agent is still searching.
\item \textit{Niche.} Environment detection resolves which niche the episode was played in.
\item \textit{Cloning, not relabeling.} When a gladiator also succeeds in a different niche, it is cloned into that niche. The original keeps its home niche.
\item \textit{Diversity.} A candidate may be a near-copy of a gladiator of the same niche, with a cosine similarity of weights above $\Res{cfg/ena/hof/similarity}$. It then replaces that twin only if its Trust is higher. Weight similarity is a cheap but imperfect proxy for behavioral similarity. It catches near-copies, such as a mutant and its parent. Two networks can still behave alike with different weights, for instance with permuted hidden units.
\item \textit{Capacity.} A candidate fills a free slot of its niche, or replaces the niche's weakest gladiator if it is better.
\end{enumerate}
This design separates what must be kept from what must keep changing.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Retention.} A gladiator is a frozen copy, and the Trust stored with it at induction does not decay. Only a candidate of its own niche with strictly higher Trust can displace it. The gladiators of one niche can thus never be evicted by those of another, however long their niche is absent. Gladiators in the population are also exempt from the evolutionary replacement and from the Trust blend.
\item \textit{Within-niche improvement.} Within a niche, the archive keeps improving. A better candidate takes a free slot or the weakest gladiator's place, and high Plasticity lowers the gate. Success in a new niche creates a clone there, instead of moving the original.
\end{itemize}
On HalfCheetah, returns vary widely with initial conditions. Admission on a single episode then risks keeping specialists that were merely lucky. There, ENA uses an \textit{Olympic} variant of the Hall of Fame. Every \Res{cfg/cheetahena/olympicsinterval} environment steps, all gladiators of the current niche replay \Res{cfg/cheetahena/olympicsepisodes} full episodes on shared seeds, an \textit{Olympiad}. A gladiator is evicted if its mean Olympic return, normalized like Trust, falls below the reference Trust. The others are confirmed, with that mean return. Each niche holds \Res{cfg/cheetahena/olympicsqualifierseats} extra seats for gladiators that have not competed yet. Only confirmed gladiators are deployed at test.

Before testing, gladiators are re-injected into the population, round-robin across niches, up to a share of \Res{cfg/ena/hof/reinject} of the population. The selection of Section~\ref{sec:selection} then chooses among them.

\subsection{Environment Detection}
\label{sec:detection}
We follow the context-detection idea of da Silva et al.~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}. Each niche $j$ owns a model of the environment's dynamics, and a niche is recognized by how well its model predicts the transitions the agent actually experiences. Our models are linear-Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1} - s_t = W_j^{\top}[s_t, a_t, 1] + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_j^2)\right).
\label{eq:dynamics}
\end{equation}
They are fitted in closed form, by ridge least squares on accumulated sufficient statistics, so the detector is gradient-free as well. We condition on the action so that the evidence does not depend on which specialist is playing, as long as the model fits the states that specialist visits. Every real transition of every episode is evidence.

\subsubsection{Detection}
The detector monitors the standardized residuals of the current niche's model. Their variance is widened by a tolerance $\tau^2$ ($\tau = \Res{cfg/ena/detect/tau}$), which absorbs the linear model's own error on nonlinear physics. The monitor is the Predictive-Mixture CuSum (PM-CuSum) of Halme, Poor and Koivunen~\cite{halme2026sequential}, a recent successor of Page's CUSUM~\cite{page1954continuous}. With $q = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ the pre-change distribution of the residuals $z_t$,
\begin{equation}
S_t = \max(S_{t-1}, 0) + \log \frac{\hat{p}_t(z_t)}{q(z_t)},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{p}_t$ is a Fixed-Share~\cite{herbster1998tracking} mixture of Gaussian predictors, fitted on sliding windows of $2, 4, \dots$ recent residuals. An alarm is raised when $S_t > \log\gamma$, with $\gamma = 10^{\Res{cfg/ena/detect/gammaexp}}$ steps. For an exact pre-change distribution, Halme et al. show that this threshold keeps the average run length to a false alarm above $\gamma$. Here, the pre-change distribution is an online-fitted linear-Gaussian approximation of nonlinear dynamics, so $\log\gamma$ is only a decision threshold. We set $\tau$ and $\gamma$ on preliminary CartPole runs, before the study, so that an unchanged environment raised no alarm. Table~\ref{tab:detection} reports the false alarms of the study itself.

We depart from the Gaussian example of~\cite{halme2026sequential} in one respect. Each window also estimates a variance, and that variance is one-sided: it is never below the pre-change variance. Our rationale is that a wrong dynamics model can change the spread of the residuals, not only their mean, and should fit worse than the right one, not better.

\subsubsection{Identification}
The recent transitions, up to \Res{cfg/ena/detect/recent}, are matched to the niche $j^{*}$ with the highest mean per-step log-likelihood. They are recognized as $j^{*}$ if it explains them within a margin of $m = \Res{cfg/ena/detect/margin}$ nats per step of a model fitted on those same transitions. Otherwise, the physics are new, and a niche is created. Only niches fitted on at least \Res{cfg/ena/detect/minsamples} transitions take part.

\subsubsection{Detect, then verify}
An alarm is only a candidate. After \Res{cfg/ena/detect/verify} post-alarm transitions, the identification test is run against the alarmed niche. If that niche still explains them, the detector treats the alarm as coming from states that its model had not yet covered. The transitions are then added to the model, and the CuSum restarts. Otherwise, a change of physics is declared, and the post-alarm transitions seed the new regime's evidence. During the test, the dynamics models are never updated. The detector still detects and identifies, but it learns nothing new.

\subsubsection{Late identity binding}
A niche is assigned only when a specialist is considered for the Hall of Fame (step~2 above). The assignment uses the transitions of the episode the specialist just played. The resolved niche's model is refined with them, or a new niche is fitted on them.

\subsubsection{Chirp detection (HalfCheetah)}
\label{sec:chirp}
On HalfCheetah, the transition models misfire. On the detection benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:detection_benchmark}, they confirm many more changes, and create many more niches, than there are environments. This happens even when a single fixed gait is played throughout. It happens more often still when the gait changes, as it does in a learning population (another gait, a flip, standing still). We did not isolate the cause. Two plausible ones are that a linear model fits the contact-rich dynamics of a running body poorly, and that its evidence is the acting specialist's own transitions.

ENA therefore plays a \textit{chirp}: a fixed probing sequence of actions, as in system identification with a known input signal~\cite{ljung1999system}. At every episode end, ENA resets the environment and plays the same \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps}-step action sequence, whatever any specialist would do. It then compares the observations this produces across niches. Probing an environment to infer its dynamics has also been learned in RL~\cite{zhou2019environment}. ENA's chirp is fixed, so its response cannot depend on the agent.

\textit{What is played.} The excitation is a multiresolution wavelet train. The \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/excitationwavelet} wavelet is played at dyadic scales, from a segment of half the chirp down to the finest. The aim is to excite both the slow and the fast responses of the body.

\textit{What is measured.} The response is the observations minus the chirp's starting observation. Each observation channel is decomposed into a \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/featurewavelet} \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/transform} tree of depth \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/level}~\cite{coifman1992entropy}. Like the discrete wavelet transform~\cite{mallat1989theory}, the tree is dyadic and non-overlapping, but it splits every band. This gives \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/bands} frequency bands of equal width, and one log-energy is kept per band. Wavelet features of a system's response to a known excitation are a standard signature of a change of physics, in structural damage detection and machine fault diagnosis~\cite{kijewski2003wavelet,peng2004application}. We compute the transforms with PyWavelets~\cite{lee2019pywavelets}. The excitation and the features were chosen by the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:detection_benchmark}, in the three HalfCheetah training environments. ENA's other HalfCheetah settings were chosen on tuning seeds disjoint from the study's replicates.

\textit{How niches are compared.} Each niche $j$ keeps the mean $\mu_j$ of its $n_j$ chirps' feature vectors. The per-feature variance $\sigma^2$ is pooled over niches, on the assumption that it comes mainly from the environment's reset noise. The mean $\bar{f}$ of the last $w = \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/window}$ chirps is tested against the current niche with
\begin{equation}
T_j = \Bigg(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d} \frac{(\bar{f}_k - \mu_{j,k})^2}{\sigma_k^2\,(1/w + 1/n_j)}\Bigg)^{1/2},
\label{eq:chirp}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the number of features. $T_j$ is about 1 under the same physics. Above the margin $\Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/margin}$, a change is confirmed, and the most recent chirps identify the new physics. They are assigned to the known niche of lowest $T_j$ within the margin, or else to a new niche (in training only). A niche is judged once it holds \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/minchirps} chirps. At test, each chirp's Gaussian log-likelihood under each niche is the dynamics evidence $L_j$ of the selection~\eqref{eq:bpr}.

The chirp costs \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps} steps per episode, \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/share}\,\% of a HalfCheetah episode, and these steps count in ENA's budget. The environment's random stream is restored after the chirp, so episode starts are unchanged. CartPole keeps the transition models, which detect its changes (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_detection}).

\subsection{Specialist Selection}
\label{sec:selection}
At test, the weights are frozen and $\mathcal{P}=0$. The only adaptation left is the choice of which gladiator acts. We make this choice with Bayesian Policy Reuse (BPR)~\cite{rosman2016bayesian}. BPR combines two kinds of evidence in one posterior: the dynamics evidence $L_j$ and the observed utility $u$. The niches play the role of BPR's types, and every gladiator is a policy. The agent keeps a belief $\beta$ over niches, and plays the gladiator of highest expected utility, $\sum_{j}\beta(j)\,\mu_{j\pi}$. Here $u = R/R_{\text{solved}}$ is the return relative to the one at which the environment counts as solved, so solving reads as $u = 1$. $\mu_{j\pi}$ is the mean utility of policy $\pi$ in niche $j$. After each episode, the belief is updated:
\begin{equation}
\beta'(j) \propto \beta(j)\; \mathcal{N}\!\left(u;\, \mu_{j\pi}, \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/noise}^2\right)\; e^{L_j}.
\label{eq:bpr}
\end{equation}
$L_j$ is the log-likelihood of the episode's transitions under niche $j$'s dynamics model~\eqref{eq:dynamics}. It is summed over the episode, with the variance widened by $\tau^2$ as in detection. On HalfCheetah, it is the chirp's log-likelihood (Section~\ref{sec:chirp}). The return term is bounded by the range of the utilities, while $L_j$ grows with the number of transitions in the episode. When the niches' models differ, the dynamics can therefore dominate the belief over niches. The returns also update the performance model $\mu_{j\pi}$, which decides which gladiator of a niche plays.

Two changes adapt BPR to our setting.
\begin{itemize}
\item BPR builds its performance model offline, by playing every policy in every type. Ours is learned online. Its prior is a utility of $\Res{cfg/ena/bpr/ownprior}$ for a gladiator in its own niche and $\Res{cfg/ena/bpr/crossprior}$ elsewhere, worth \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/prior} observation. Every observed utility then updates it, weighted by the posterior. On HalfCheetah, the solved return is out of reach. There, a gladiator's own-niche prior is its mean Olympic return relative to $R_{\text{solved}}$, and its prior elsewhere is $\Res{cfg/ena/bpr/crossprior}$ times that.
\item BPR assumes one fixed type per trial. To follow a changing environment, a Fixed-Share step~\cite{herbster1998tracking} mixes back a uniform share after each update: $\beta \leftarrow \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/mix}\,\beta' + \Res{cfg/ena/bpr/switch}/|\mathcal{J}|$. A confirmed change of physics also resets the belief to uniform.
\end{itemize}
At test, the belief starts uniform when the test begins. It is reset to uniform only when the detector confirms a change of physics. As in training, the test environments follow one another unannounced. Three quantities still adapt at test: the belief, the selection's performance model, and the detector's alarms. The weights, Plasticity ($\mathcal{P}=0$) and the detector's models stay fixed. A confirmed change still resets the input statistics. This matters only for a specialist outside the archive, because a gladiator keeps the statistics it was archived with.

Without a Hall of Fame, there is no gladiator to choose from. The agent then plays a random member of its final population in each test episode. This is how the ablation's variants without a Hall of Fame are tested (Section~\ref{sec:variants}). Section~\ref{sec:selection_ablation} compares BPR with two simpler rules: deploying the highest-Trust gladiator of the niche the detector recognizes, and deploying the highest-Trust gladiator overall.
