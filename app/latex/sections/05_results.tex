\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section first evaluates the individual contributions of ENA's constituent skills and its specialist selection mechanism through ablation studies on CartPole. Performance is subsequently analyzed on previously encountered and unseen environments alongside single-policy baselines, accompanied by the detection benchmarks on HalfCheetah. Finally, the interaction costs of failure-driven search and alternative controller formulations are assessed. Table~\ref{tab:rq_map} maps each subsection to the research question it informs. Reporting conventions and statistical tests are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:metrics}; unless stated otherwise, reported values are means over independent replicates with standard deviations in parentheses, and significance is assessed via Welch's two-sided $t$-test.

\subsection{Shape of the Plasticity Controller}
\label{sec:controller}
The linear ramp controller~\eqref{eq:ramp} was compared with the two alternatives from Section~\ref{sec:controllers}: the quadratic and the fuzzy controller. Each alternative replaced only ENA-Combined's Plasticity controller, and ran for \Res{controller/n} replicates with the ablation's seeds. Every agent of a replicate starts from the same random state. \subsection{Selection Ablation}
\label{sec:selection_ablation}
ENA-Combined deploys its archived specialists at test with Bayesian policy reuse (Section~\ref{sec:selection}). Two simpler drivers replaced only that choice. \textit{Niche match} plays the highest-Trust gladiator of the niche the detector currently recognizes. When that niche has no gladiator, it plays the highest-Trust gladiator overall. It ignores the test returns. \textit{Best Trust} always plays the highest-Trust gladiator overall, ignoring both the detector and the returns.

Training selection is the same for every driver. Each alternative therefore ran for \Res{selection/n} replicates with the ablation's seeds, and the ablation's ENA-Combined is the Bayesian reference without being re-run. In \Res{selection/identity/identical} of the \Res{selection/identity/checked} alternative runs, the training log is byte-identical to the reference's. Two comparisons, fixed before the runs, form one Holm family: Bayesian policy reuse against niche match, and niche match against best Trust. At \Res{selection/n} replicates, the family has \Res{stats/power}\% power for $d \ge \Res{selection/mde}$.

\subsection{Environment Detection on HalfCheetah}
\label{sec:detection_benchmark}
The detector's inputs are the agent's own, so detection can be evaluated without training. The detection benchmark plays scripted episodes in Earth, Heavy-Rough and Mars-Slippery, \Res{cfg/detbench/episodes} in each. Every \Res{cfg/detbench/block} episodes, it changes the acting policy among near-still, uniformly random and sinusoidal gaits. The same family of gaits is used in every environment, so that only the physics differ. The benchmark feeds the detector exactly the calls ENA makes, with a niche assignment after every episode. A run meets the target when three conditions hold. It confirms both switches within \Res{cfg/detbench/window} episodes. It raises no false alarm inside an environment. It gives each environment its own niche, covering at least \Res{cfg/detbench/purity}\,\% of that environment's episodes.

Each detector was evaluated on \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} independent benchmark runs. Each run has its own random seed, which draws different policies and resets, so each run is identified by its seed. Intervals are exact (Clopper--Pearson) at \Res{stats/ci}\,\%. Comparisons are exact McNemar tests on seed-paired outcomes, Holm-adjusted as one family. As a check of the benchmark's criteria, CartPole's transition-model detector meets the target on \Res{detbench/cartpole/passes} of \Res{detbench/cartpole/seeds} runs of the corresponding CartPole benchmark.

On HalfCheetah, the transition-model detector is calibrated for the domain: margin $m = \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/noveltymargin}$, at least \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/minsamples} transitions to fit a niche, and \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/minverifysamples} to verify an alarm. It meets the target on \Res{detbench/transition/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} runs. On average, it confirms \Res{detbench/transition/changes} changes, \Res{detbench/transition/inside} of them inside an environment, and creates \Res{detbench/transition/niches} niches. Only \Res{detbench/transition/purity}\,\% of the worst environment's episodes fall in its niche. With one fixed gait in every environment, the same detector still confirms \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/changes} changes and creates \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/niches} niches on average. It also merges two environments into one niche on \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/merged} of the \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} seeds.

Table~\ref{tab:chirp_ablation} ablates the chirp's excitation and response features over \Res{detbench/seeds} benchmark seeds. Each cell counts the seeds on which that pair meets the target, and ENA's chirp is the best of them. The Mexican-hat wavelet train is the best excitation. It meets the target on at least \Res{detbench/chosenexcitation/minpasses} of the \Res{detbench/seeds} seeds with every response feature, and on every seed with \Res{detbench/passing} of them. No linear frequency sweep, sine or impulse meets the target on any seed. The best configuration without a wavelet train, an \Res{detbench/bestother/excitation} with \Res{detbench/bestother/features} features, meets it on \Res{detbench/bestother/passes} seeds. The Haar and db4~\cite{daubechies1988orthonormal} trains depend strongly on the features. ENA uses the Mexican-hat train with Haar wavelet packets (in bold), which meets the target on every seed.

On the confirmation runs, this chirp meets the target on \Res{detbench/chosen/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} seeds (\Res{stats/ci}\,\% interval \Res{detbench/chosen/lower}--\Res{detbench/chosen/upper}\,\%). It confirms a false change on \Res{detbench/chosen/falseseeds} of them, and confirms every switch at the end of the first episode after it. The share of the worst environment's episodes that fall in its own niche averages \Res{detbench/chosen/puritymean}\,\% over the seeds, and is never below \Res{detbench/chosen/minpurity}\,\%. The chirp meets the target more often than the transition-model detector (\Res{detbench/cmp/chirp/p}). On the \Res{detbench/seeds} ablation seeds it was chosen on, it also meets the target more often than the best configuration without a wavelet train (\Res{detbench/cmp/other/p}).

In the HalfCheetah study itself (Section~\ref{sec:cheetah}), the chirp gives each training environment its own niche in \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/recog/distinct} of the \Res{cheetah/n} replicates (Table~\ref{tab:cheetah_detection}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_detection}). A training run, with two switches, confirms \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/detect/train/changes/total} changes on average. A test, with four switches, confirms \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/detect/test/changes/total}. At test, the training environments are recognized as their own niches in most replicates: Earth-Standard in \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/recog/Earth-Standard/Earth-Standard}, Heavy-Rough in \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/recog/Heavy-Rough/Heavy-Rough} and Mars-Slippery in \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/recog/Mars-Slippery/Mars-Slippery}. High-Wind is recognized as Earth-Standard's niche in \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/recog/High-Wind/Earth-Standard} replicates.
