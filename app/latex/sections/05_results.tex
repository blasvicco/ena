\subsection{Environment Detection on HalfCheetah}
\label{sec:detection_benchmark}
The detector's inputs are the agent's own, so detection can be evaluated without training. The detection benchmark plays scripted episodes in Earth, Heavy-Rough and Mars-Slippery (\Res{cfg/detbench/episodes} each), changing the acting policy every \Res{cfg/detbench/block} episodes among near-still, uniformly random and sinusoidal gaits, the same family in every environment so that only the physics differ, and feeds the detector exactly the calls ENA makes, with a niche assignment after every episode. A run meets the target when it confirms both switches within \Res{cfg/detbench/window} episodes, triggers no false alarms inside an environment, and gives each environment its own niche covering at least \Res{cfg/detbench/purity}\,\% of its episodes. Each detector is evaluated across \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} independent benchmark runs (seeds), each drawing different policies and resets. Intervals are exact (Clopper--Pearson) at \Res{stats/ci}\,\%; comparisons are exact McNemar tests on seed-paired outcomes, Holm-adjusted as one family. As a check of the benchmark criteria, CartPole's transition-model detector meets the target on \Res{detbench/cartpole/passes} of \Res{detbench/cartpole/seeds} runs on the corresponding CartPole benchmark.

On HalfCheetah, the transition-model detector, with settings calibrated for HalfCheetah, meets the target on \Res{detbench/transition/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} runs: it confirms an average of \Res{detbench/transition/changes} changes (\Res{detbench/transition/inside} of them inside an environment) and creates an average of \Res{detbench/transition/niches} niches, and only \Res{detbench/transition/purity}\,\% of the worst environment's episodes fall in its niche. With one fixed gait in every environment the same detector still confirms \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/changes} changes and creates \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/niches} niches on average, and merges two environments into one niche on \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/merged} of the seeds.

Table~\ref{tab:chirp_ablation} ablates the chirp's excitation and response features across \Res{detbench/seeds} benchmark seeds. The Mexican-hat wavelet train meets the target on at least \Res{detbench/chosenexcitation/minpasses} of the \Res{detbench/seeds} seeds with every response feature, and on every seed with \Res{detbench/passing} of them. No linear chirp, sine or impulse meets it on any seed, and the best configuration without a wavelet train, an \Res{detbench/bestother/excitation} with \Res{detbench/bestother/features} features, on \Res{detbench/bestother/passes} seeds. The Haar and db4~\cite{daubechies1988orthonormal} trains depend strongly on the features, the db4 train with a Haar DWT meeting the target on only \Res{detbench/screened/passes} seeds. ENA uses the Mexican-hat train with Haar wavelet packets (bold), which meets it on \Res{detbench/chosengrid/passes} of the \Res{detbench/seeds} seeds.

On the confirmation runs, this chirp meets the target on \Res{detbench/chosen/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} seeds (\Res{stats/ci}\,\% interval \Res{detbench/chosen/lower}--\Res{detbench/chosen/upper}\,\%): it confirms a false change on \Res{detbench/chosen/falseseeds} of them, catches every switch with a delay of at most \Res{detbench/chosen/maxdelay} episodes, and keeps at least \Res{detbench/chosen/minpurity}\,\% of every environment's episodes in its niche. It meets the target more often than the transition-model detector (\Res{detbench/cmp/chirp/p}), and on the ablation seeds more often than the best configuration without a wavelet train (\Res{detbench/cmp/other/p}).
