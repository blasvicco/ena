\subsection{Compute Cost of Plasticity}
\label{sec:compute}
Variants with Plasticity train in \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity/time/min/mean}--\Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/time/min/mean} minutes per replicate, far longer than those without it (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); candidate evaluation dominates the cost.

We ran ENA-Combined once with the full controller ($\mathcal{P}=1$, Section~\ref{sec:controllers}) on the ablation's first replicate, and compared it with the same replicate under the linear ramp (ENA-Combined) and under the zero controller (ENA-HoF+EnvDetection). The three runs spent \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/compute/candsteps/mean}, \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/compute/candsteps/mean} and \Res{compute/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/compute/candsteps/mean} environment steps evaluating candidates during training: full search costs \Res{compute/ratio/full-ramp} times the linear ramp, which costs \Res{compute/ratio/ramp-zero} times the minimum. At full effort every generation evaluates every other specialist for up to $s_{\max}$ steps, so its cost is fixed by construction; the linear ramp's cost varies across replicates (standard deviation \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/compute/candsteps/std} steps over the ablation's \Res{ablation/n}), so these ratios are this replicate's.

Test reliability in this replicate was \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/test/rel/mean}\% at full effort, \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% with the ramp, and \Res{compute/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/mean}\% at minimum search. Most of the gap between full effort and the ramp comes from Brown-dwarf, the extrapolation environment: a mean return of \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/test/Brown-dwarf/mean} against \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/test/Brown-dwarf/mean}. A single replicate supports no test: among the ramp's \Res{compute/rank/of} ablation replicates, \Res{compute/rank/atleast} reached at least the reliability of full search, and this replicate is one of the ramp's weaker runs. Whether the ramp matches the reliability of full search is therefore open.

\subsection{Shape of the Plasticity Controller}
\label{sec:controller}
ENA's controller, the linear ramp~\eqref{eq:ramp}, was compared with the two alternatives of Section~\ref{sec:controllers}, the quadratic and the fuzzy controller, each replacing only ENA-Combined's Plasticity controller. For each metric, the two comparisons with the ramp form one Holm family; at \Res{controller/n} replicates, each has \Res{stats/power}\% power for $d \ge \Res{controller/mde}$. \subsection{Environment Detection on HalfCheetah}
\label{sec:detection_benchmark}
The detector's inputs are the agent's own, so detection can be evaluated without training. The detection benchmark plays scripted episodes in Earth, Heavy-Rough and Mars-Slippery (\Res{cfg/detbench/episodes} each), changing the acting policy every \Res{cfg/detbench/block} episodes among near-still, uniformly random and sinusoidal gaits, the same family in every environment so that only the physics differ, and feeds the detector exactly the calls ENA makes, with a niche assignment after every episode. A run meets the target when it confirms both switches within \Res{cfg/detbench/window} episodes, triggers no false alarms inside an environment, and gives each environment its own niche covering at least \Res{cfg/detbench/purity}\,\% of its episodes. Each detector is evaluated across \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} independent benchmark runs (seeds), each drawing different policies and resets. Intervals are exact (Clopper--Pearson) at \Res{stats/ci}\,\%; comparisons are exact McNemar tests on seed-paired outcomes, Holm-adjusted as one family. As a check of the benchmark criteria, CartPole's transition-model detector meets the target on \Res{detbench/cartpole/passes} of \Res{detbench/cartpole/seeds} runs on the corresponding CartPole benchmark.

On HalfCheetah, the transition-model detector, calibrated for HalfCheetah (margin $m = \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/noveltymargin}$, at least \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/minsamples} transitions to fit a niche and \Res{cfg/detbench/transition/minverifysamples} to verify an alarm), meets the target on \Res{detbench/transition/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} runs: it confirms an average of \Res{detbench/transition/changes} changes (\Res{detbench/transition/inside} of them inside an environment) and creates an average of \Res{detbench/transition/niches} niches, and only \Res{detbench/transition/purity}\,\% of the worst environment's episodes fall in its niche. With one fixed gait in every environment the same detector still confirms \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/changes} changes and creates \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/niches} niches on average, and merges two environments into one niche on \Res{detbench/transitionfixed/merged} of the \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} seeds.

Table~\ref{tab:chirp_ablation} ablates the chirp's excitation and response features across \Res{detbench/seeds} benchmark seeds, and the best of them is ENA's chirp. The Mexican-hat wavelet train is the best excitation: it meets the target on at least \Res{detbench/chosenexcitation/minpasses} of the \Res{detbench/seeds} seeds with every response feature, and on every seed with \Res{detbench/passing} of them. No linear frequency sweep, sine or impulse meets it on any seed, and the best configuration without a wavelet train, an \Res{detbench/bestother/excitation} with \Res{detbench/bestother/features} features, on \Res{detbench/bestother/passes} seeds. The Haar and db4~\cite{daubechies1988orthonormal} trains depend strongly on the features. ENA uses the Mexican-hat train with Haar wavelet packets (bold), which meets the target on every seed.

On the confirmation runs, this chirp meets the target on \Res{detbench/chosen/passes} of \Res{cfg/detbench/confirmseeds} seeds (\Res{stats/ci}\,\% interval \Res{detbench/chosen/lower}--\Res{detbench/chosen/upper}\,\%). It confirms a false change on \Res{detbench/chosen/falseseeds} of them and confirms every switch at the end of the first episode after it. The share of the worst environment's episodes that fall in its own niche averages \Res{detbench/chosen/puritymean}\,\% over the seeds and is never below \Res{detbench/chosen/minpurity}\,\%. The chirp meets the target more often than the transition-model detector (\Res{detbench/cmp/chirp/p}), and, on the \Res{detbench/seeds} ablation seeds it was chosen on, more often than the best configuration without a wavelet train (\Res{detbench/cmp/other/p}).
