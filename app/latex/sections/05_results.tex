\section{Results and Performance Analysis}
\label{sec:results}


All numbers below are means over independent replicates, with the standard deviation in parentheses where relevant; $p$ is the two-sided Welch's $t$-test.

\subsection{Skill Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation_test} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_reliability} report the frozen test over \Res{ablation/n} replicates. With all three skills, \textbf{ENA-Combined} reaches a test reliability of \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% (\Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/std}), against \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% (\Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/std}) without any skill, and is better than every other variant. Its smallest margin, over ENA-Plasticity, is \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/diff} points (\Res{stats/ci}\% CI \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/high}; $d = \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/d}$; \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/p}).

Adding the skills one at a time shows how they depend on each other. Differences are in reliability points, with their \Res{stats/ci}\% interval and Holm-corrected $p$-value:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Plasticity is the largest single contribution}: \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-Plasticity/test/rel/diff} over ENA-NoSkill (\Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-Plasticity/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-Plasticity/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-Plasticity/test/rel/pholm}), and \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/diff} on top of the other two skills (\Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/pholm}). Searching in proportion to failure is what produces good specialists in the first place.
\item \textit{An archive alone does not help}: the Hall of Fame changes ENA-NoSkill's reliability by \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-HoF/test/rel/diff} (\Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-HoF/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-HoF/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-HoF/test/rel/pholm}) and ENA-Plasticity's by \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/diff} (\Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/pholm}). Without knowing which physics a specialist was good for, the agent cannot tell which one to recall.
\item \textit{Environment detection turns the archive into a memory}: organized by niche, the archive gains \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/diff} without Plasticity (\Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/pholm}), and \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity+HoF-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/diff} with it (\Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity+HoF-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity+HoF-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity+HoF-vs-ENA-Combined/test/rel/pholm}). Detection pays off once Plasticity has produced specialists worth remembering.
\end{itemize}
The full combination also removes most of the dependence on the initial population: the standard deviation of reliability across replicates drops from \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF/test/rel/std}--\Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/std} points for the archive-only variants to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/std}. On the two environments never seen in training, ENA-Combined's mean return is \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/Neptune/mean} on Neptune, whose physics its detector recognizes as Earth's (Appendix~\ref{app:information}). On Brown-dwarf it is \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/Brown-dwarf/mean} (\Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/Brown-dwarf/std}). Brown-dwarf's gravity is twice Jupiter's, and its physics are recognized as no known niche, so ENA-Combined does not solve it reliably.

\textit{Statistical power.} Every effect the conclusions rest on is resolved. Plasticity, environment detection and their combination each have $d$ between \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/d} and \Res{ablation/ENA-HoF-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/d}, every one of their intervals excludes zero, and ENA-Combined's margin over each variant does too.

The two Hall of Fame effects without detection are not resolved in size. On its own, the archive lowers reliability: the interval excludes zero, but measuring the size with \Res{stats/power}\% power would take \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill-vs-ENA-HoF/test/rel/nreq} replicates. On top of Plasticity, the effect ($d = \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/d}$) would take \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/nreq}.

We did not add replicates for them, for three reasons:
\begin{itemize}
\item Neither is a claim of this paper. The Hall of Fame is proposed together with environment detection, and that effect is resolved.
\item The replicate count was fixed in advance, and extending it because of a result already seen inflates the false-positive rate.
\item The interval already answers the design question. Without detection, the archive does not raise ENA-Plasticity's reliability by more than \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/test/rel/ci/high} points.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compute Cost of Plasticity}
\label{sec:compute}
Plasticity is also the expensive skill: variants with it train in \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity/time/min/mean}--\Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/time/min/mean} minutes per replicate, against \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/time/min/mean}--\Res{ablation/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/time/min/mean} without it, because candidate evaluation dominates the cost. Scaling the search to the failure signal with the linear ramp, instead of always searching at full effort, is what keeps that cost bounded.

We ran ENA-Combined once with the full controller ($\mathcal{P}=1$, Section~\ref{sec:controllers}), and compared it with the same seeded replicate under the linear ramp (ENA-Combined) and under the zero controller (ENA-HoF+EnvDetection). The three runs spent \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/compute/candsteps/mean}, \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/compute/candsteps/mean} and \Res{compute/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/compute/candsteps/mean} environment steps evaluating candidates during training. Full search therefore costs \Res{compute/ratio/full-ramp} times the linear ramp, which in turn costs \Res{compute/ratio/ramp-zero} times the minimum.

One replicate is enough for this measurement. At full effort every generation evaluates every other specialist for up to $s_{\max}$ steps, so its cost is set by construction and varies only where a candidate's episode ends early. The linear ramp's cost does vary from run to run, with a standard deviation of \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/compute/candsteps/std} steps over the ablation's \Res{ablation/n} replicates.

Test reliability in this replicate was \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/test/rel/mean}\% at full effort, \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% with the ramp, and \Res{compute/ENA-HoF+EnvDetection/test/rel/mean}\% without search. Most of the gap between full effort and the ramp comes from Brown-dwarf, the extrapolation environment: a mean return of \Res{compute/ENA-Combined-PlasticityFull/test/Brown-dwarf/mean} against \Res{compute/ENA-Combined/test/Brown-dwarf/mean}.

A single run supports no test of reliability, so we place it among the ramp's own replicates instead. \Res{compute/rank/atleast} of the ramp's \Res{compute/rank/of} ablation replicates reached at least the reliability of full search, and this replicate is one of the ramp's weaker runs. Full search's result is therefore one the ramp reaches in about half its runs, with \Res{compute/ratio/full-ramp} times less candidate evaluation.

\subsection{Shape of the Plasticity Controller}
\label{sec:controller}
ENA's controller, the linear ramp~\eqref{eq:ramp}, is one of several ways to turn a return into a search effort. We compared it with the two alternatives of Section~\ref{sec:controllers}, the quadratic and the fuzzy controller, each replacing only ENA-Combined's Plasticity controller.

Each alternative ran for \Res{controller/n} replicates with the ablation's seeds. Every agent of a replicate starts from the same random state, so the ablation's ENA-Combined is the linear-ramp run of the same replicate. It serves as the reference without being re-run.

The decision rule was fixed before the runs. An alternative replaces the ramp if its test reliability is significantly higher, or not significantly lower with significantly fewer candidate-evaluation steps. Both comparisons with the ramp form one family (Holm). At \Res{controller/n} replicates, this family has \Res{stats/power}\% power for $d \ge \Res{controller/mde}$.

The results, with the \Res{stats/ci}\% interval and the Holm-corrected $p$-value of each difference:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Linear ramp (reference):} test reliability \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% (\Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/std}), with \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/compute/candsteps/mean} candidate steps.
\item \textit{Fuzzy:} \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/mean}\% (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/std}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Reliability: \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/diff} points (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/pholm}).
  \item Cost: \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/compute/candsteps/diff} more candidate steps (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/compute/candsteps/ci/low} to \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/compute/candsteps/ci/high}; \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/compute/candsteps/pholm}), for \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/compute/candsteps/mean} in total.
  \end{itemize}
  The fuzzy controller searches more and gains nothing measurable. Its interval rules out a gain of more than \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-FuzzyPlasticity/test/rel/ci/high} points.
\item \textit{Quadratic:} \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/mean}\% (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/std}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Reliability: \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/diff} points (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/ci/low} to \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/ci/high}; \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/pholm}).
  \item Cost: \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/compute/candsteps/diff} candidate steps (\Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/compute/candsteps/ci/low} to \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/compute/candsteps/ci/high}; \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/compute/candsteps/pholm}).
  \end{itemize}
  The quadratic controller was more reliable and cheaper in this sample, but neither difference is significant. The observed effect ($d = \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/d}$) is below the detectable one, and resolving it with \Res{stats/power}\% power would take about \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-vs-ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/nreq} replicates per controller.
\end{itemize}

By the rule, the controller kept is the \Res{controller/verdict}. We do not claim that the ramp is better than the quadratic controller: the data leave that open, and the interval of their difference includes both no difference and a gain of over ten points for the quadratic controller.

As an exploratory observation, not part of the planned comparison, the quadratic controller's reliability varied less across replicates (standard deviation \Res{controller/ENA-Combined-QuadraticPlasticity/test/rel/std} against \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/std}). Settling the question would need a separate study, planned for that effect size on fresh seeds. Adding replicates to this one after seeing the result would inflate its false-positive rate.

\subsubsection{Adaptability}
ENA does not reach the success threshold first. PPO first solves each training environment in \Res{challenge/PPO/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/mean}, \Res{challenge/PPO/adapt/Jupiter-Heavy:steps/mean} and \Res{challenge/PPO/adapt/Earth-Standard:steps/mean} steps, ENA in \Res{challenge/ENA-Combined/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/mean}, \Res{challenge/ENA-Combined/adapt/Jupiter-Heavy:steps/mean} and \Res{challenge/ENA-Combined/adapt/Earth-Standard:steps/mean} (\Res{challenge/PPO-vs-ENA-Combined/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/p}, \Res{challenge/PPO-vs-ENA-Combined/adapt/Jupiter-Heavy:steps/p} and \Res{challenge/PPO-vs-ENA-Combined/adapt/Earth-Standard:steps/p}). Against the other two baselines the picture is mixed (Table~\ref{tab:challenge_adapt}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Mars:} ENA solves it sooner than DQN (\Res{challenge/DQN/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/mean} steps; \Res{challenge/DQN-vs-ENA-Combined/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/p}) and as soon as SAC-Discrete (\Res{challenge/SAC-Discrete/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/mean}; \Res{challenge/SAC-Discrete-vs-ENA-Combined/adapt/Mars-Light:steps/p}).

ENA's search spends steps on many candidates before one is good enough. What it gains in exchange is that, once found, a solution is kept.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item ENA's dynamics models are linear. They separate the CartPole environments, but physics a linear model cannot tell apart (such as Earth and Neptune) share a niche; richer models may be needed in other domains.
\item ENA solves a new environment later than PPO, the price of a gradient-free search.
\item The compute comparison is in wall-clock time on one machine, with our own implementations of every agent.
\item The compute-cost study of Plasticity is a single replicate per setting.
