\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA), a gradient-free agent for non-stationary environments that keeps a memory of specialists organized by the physics it detects. On non-stationary CartPole, its skills proved complementary: searching in proportion to failure produces good specialists, and detecting and recognizing environments turns the archive of those specialists into a memory the agent can use. Together they raise test reliability from \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% over \Res{ablation/n} replicates. At an equal budget of environment steps, ENA retained an environment that DQN, PPO and SAC-Discrete forgot, and reached a higher test reliability in less wall-clock time. It was, however, slower than PPO to solve each new environment for the first time, and SAC-Discrete scored slightly higher on the environments it did not forget: ENA trades speed of first adaptation and a few points of peak return for retention. On continuous-control HalfCheetah the same trade-off appears in a sharper form: ENA retains \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/retention/left/mean}\% of its performance on the environments it trained on first, against \Res{cheetah/PPO/retention/left/mean}\% for PPO and \Res{cheetah/SAC/retention/left/mean}\% for SAC; SAC, however, reaches substantially higher absolute returns within each environment, because ENA can play only \Res{cheetah/ENA-Combined/episodes/perenv} learning episodes per environment on the same step budget.

Several directions follow. The linear dynamics models separate our environments, but physics a linear model cannot tell apart share a niche; nonlinear models, still fitted without gradients, would sharpen identification in richer domains. For continuous physical systems that cannot be reset at episode boundaries, active spectral identification could be extended by superimposing low-amplitude perturbation dither signals directly onto operational control actions, or by training posture-independent probing reflexes. ENA's search operators are deliberately simple, and the evolution strategies that Nisioti et al.~\cite{nisioti2026continual} found to balance adaptation and forgetting well could replace them, with environment detection and the Hall of Fame on top. Specialist selection could also be learned: a contextual bandit that maps the detector's evidence to the specialist to deploy, instead of the fixed performance model of Bayesian policy reuse. A promising hybrid direction is combining gradient-free neuroevolution with gradient-based learners: gradient updates (such as SAC or PPO) could act as fast intra-niche policy optimizers to accelerate initial adaptation and reach higher peak returns within an environment, while ENA's population dynamics, Plasticity, and physics-indexed Hall of Fame protect against catastrophic forgetting across non-stationary regime switches. Finally, the population's evaluations are independent and parallelize naturally on accelerators, which would allow larger populations and longer non-stationary streams.
