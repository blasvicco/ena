\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA), a gradient-free agent for non-stationary environments that keeps a memory of specialists organized by the physics it detects. On non-stationary CartPole, its skills proved complementary: searching in proportion to failure produces good specialists, and detecting and recognizing environments turns the archive of those specialists into a memory the agent can use. Together they raise test reliability from \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\% over \Res{ablation/n} replicates. At an equal budget of environment steps, ENA retained an environment that DQN, PPO and SAC-Discrete forgot, and reached a higher test reliability in less wall-clock time. It was, however, slower than PPO to solve each new environment for the first time, and SAC-Discrete scored slightly higher on the environments it did not forget: ENA trades speed of first adaptation and a few points of peak return for retention. \todo{HalfCheetah: one or two sentences on the continuous-control results.}

Several directions follow. The linear dynamics models separate our environments, but physics a linear model cannot tell apart share a niche; nonlinear models, still fitted without gradients, would sharpen identification in richer domains. ENA's search operators are deliberately simple, and the evolution strategies that Nisioti et al.~\cite{nisioti2026continual} found to balance adaptation and forgetting well could replace them, with environment detection and the Hall of Fame on top. Specialist selection could also be learned: a contextual bandit that maps the detector's evidence to the specialist to deploy, instead of the fixed performance model of Bayesian policy reuse. Finally, the population's evaluations are independent and parallelize naturally on accelerators, which would allow larger populations and longer non-stationary streams.
