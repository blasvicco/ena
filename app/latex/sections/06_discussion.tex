\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}

\textit{RQ1: do the skills mitigate forgetting? Supported on CartPole, for the tested sequences.} In the ablation, the variants that learn Mars and Jupiter are those with Plasticity. Among them, only ENA-Combined keeps both environments at test, and it is the most reliable variant (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). Adding the archive also changes how the test policy is chosen, so the ablation does not isolate the archive's contribution from the selection's. Without Plasticity, ENA barely learns Jupiter at all. The ablation's gain in reliability therefore combines learning and keeping. The single-policy learners lose most of Jupiter, the CartPole environment trained just before the last, though they keep Mars. On HalfCheetah, they keep \Res{cheetah/SAC/retention/left/mean}--\Res{cheetah/PPO/retention/left/mean}\% on average of what they reached on the two environments left behind, while ENA keeps all of it (Sections~\ref{sec:forgetting_cartpole} and~\ref{sec:cheetah}). HalfCheetah has no skill ablation, so its result is consistent with RQ1 but does not show which skill produces it. These findings concern the evaluated implementations, task sequences and budgets.

\textit{RQ1a: is an archive enough by itself? No.} Variants with a Hall of Fame but no detection are not more reliable than the same variants without it, and ENA-HoF is less reliable than ENA-NoSkill. Variants whose archive is organized by detected physics are more reliable than those with an archive but no detection, in both cases. With Plasticity, they are also more reliable than the variant without any archive, and only they keep both environments learned (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). In this ablation, keeping proven specialists was not enough: the archive came with higher reliability only when it also recorded the physics each specialist was proven in. The recall rule also matters. Bayesian policy reuse is more reliable than deploying the recognized niche's highest-Trust gladiator. Niche match, in turn, is not significantly more reliable than ignoring the niche (Section~\ref{sec:selection_ablation}).

\textit{RQ1b: does the memory cost extra interaction? Partly answered.} Without Plasticity, archiving and detecting add no measurable candidate evaluation of their own. Their only measured cost is wall-clock time (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). With Plasticity, adding the Hall of Fame comes with more candidate steps. The increase is not significant, but the data cannot exclude it, up to \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity-vs-ENA-Plasticity+HoF/compute/candsteps/ci/high} steps per replicate. The archive takes no extra candidate evaluation to fill, but it may change how much the failure-driven search spends.

\textit{RQ1c: can the memory be built online, without labels? Yes in both domains, with different drivers.} On CartPole, the transition-model detector separates the training environments, with the fragmentation, false alarms and merged physics of Table~\ref{tab:detection}. On HalfCheetah, the same kind of detector fails (Section~\ref{sec:chirp}). The chirp, whose response does not depend on the agent, meets the detection target on the benchmark. It also gives each training environment its own niche in every replicate of the HalfCheetah study (Section~\ref{sec:detection_benchmark}). Neither driver uses a gradient.

\textit{RQ2: does failure-driven search pay? Partly supported.} The comparisons that add Plasticity show the largest differences in the ablation, and only the variants with Plasticity learn the harder environments (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). In the ablation, however, the budget is a number of episodes, so more search also means far more interaction with the environment. Searching at full effort in every episode costs an order of magnitude more candidate evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:compute}), a cost set by construction. Whether the ramp reaches what full effort does remains open. In the single replicate run at full effort, full effort was the more reliable. Whether scaling search to failure beats spreading the same effort evenly is untested. The quadratic controller was not significantly worse than the ramp, and may be better (Section~\ref{sec:controller}). These findings therefore concern the principle of scaling search to failure, not the linear shape we use.

\subsection{How Detection May Help the Archive}
We read RQ1a's result as a problem of recall rather than storage, an interpretation the ablation does not test directly. Without niches, the archive is one pool of gladiators proven in different physics. During training, their protected copies hold population slots, whatever the current physics. At test, the selection has no physical evidence with which to tell a Jupiter specialist from an Earth one. Adding the archive also replaces a random population member by Bayesian policy reuse (Section~\ref{sec:variants}). With detection, every gladiator carries the physics it was proven in. The dynamics likelihoods of the selection~\eqref{eq:bpr} point it to the matching niche, and the gladiators of one niche can no longer be evicted by another niche's.

Selection rules that use the same niches also differ in reliability. Bayesian policy reuse differs from niche match in two ways. It weighs each episode's dynamics likelihoods into a belief, instead of following the detector's single decision. It also learns from the test returns. The selection ablation does not separate the two. One possible explanation, which we did not test, involves the returns. They let Bayesian policy reuse abandon a gladiator that fails in its own niche, which niche match cannot do, and over a test niche match tries far fewer individuals (Section~\ref{sec:selection_ablation}). A gladiator archived for solving a niche could still fail there at test, and without evidence from the returns, a failing one would keep playing.

\subsection{Retention, Adaptation and Absolute Return}
\label{sec:retention}
ENA keeps what it learns, but it falls behind the single-policy learners elsewhere, by an amount that differs by domain. We did not test what causes these gaps. On CartPole, ENA solves a new environment later than PPO. One possible reason is that its search spends steps evaluating many candidates before one is good enough. SAC-Discrete scores slightly higher on the environments it does not forget, while ENA keeps the one environment that the single-policy learners lose.

On HalfCheetah, the gap is larger. ENA keeps what it learned, but it learns far less than SAC within the same budget. Most of its steps go to evaluating candidates rather than to its acting specialist's episodes (Section~\ref{sec:forgetting_protocol}), and the gradient-free search itself may also limit what it learns. Our experiments do not separate these possible causes, nor rule out others. What SAC loses on the environments left behind exceeds the return ENA reached there at the end of each phase (Section~\ref{sec:cheetah}). We did not test why ENA's retention exceeds 100\%. One possible explanation is that its end-of-phase training returns include the episodes in which the $\epsilon$-greedy selection plays a random non-archived specialist, and specialists not yet confirmed by an Olympiad, whereas the test plays only Olympic-confirmed gladiators, chosen by Bayesian policy reuse.

We expect a specialist archive to be most useful when environments recur and relearning them is expensive, but we did not test this. When an environment is unlikely to return, or a gradient learner can relearn it quickly, a single strong policy that is partly forgotten may be worth more than a weaker one kept intact.

\subsection{Scope of the Comparison}
\label{sec:scope}
The single-policy learners measure how much standard learners forget on these streams. The claim of this paper rests on the skill ablation. The comparison has two limits. First, at test, every agent's weights are frozen, but ENA still routes among its archived specialists with an updating belief. The single-policy learners deploy one policy. The comparison thus contrasts context-indexed specialist preservation with single-policy learning, not two equally equipped systems.

Second, the continual-RL methods closest to ENA are not baselines here, for reasons that differ between them. OWL~\cite{kessler2022same} and TeLAPA~\cite{lillo2026beyond} are told where tasks begin and end. OWL trains the head of the task it is given, and infers the task only at test. TeLAPA builds a task's archive, and retrieves a starting point from its archives, at each task boundary it is given. Both build their memory during the stream, but from labeled tasks. On our streams, they would need either the task boundaries, which no agent here receives, or a detector to supply them. Supplying one would amount to adding ENA's environment-detection skill to them. ITE~\cite{cully2015robots} builds its archive offline, before deployment. It adapts once to each damage, starting when performance drops below a user-set threshold. It neither keeps a memory across a sequence of conditions, nor recognizes a condition that returns.

RL-CD~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}, MBCD~\cite{alegre2021minimum} and SWOKS~\cite{dick2024statistical} do detect contexts online without labels, and keep a policy per context, so they address the same problem. A comparison with them, however, would be confounded. RL-CD's models are tabular and need a discretized state space, while MBCD and SWOKS learn by gradient descent. A difference could therefore come from the learning algorithm rather than from the memory. We leave this comparison to the hybrid ENA of Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}, which can share their learner. A gradient-trained policy per niche, with ENA's own detector and selection, would also show whether the skills mitigate forgetting beyond ENA's evolutionary loop.

Finally, the wall-clock times are measured with our own implementations, on one machine for CartPole and on one cloud instance per replicate for HalfCheetah. Environment steps and candidate steps are properties of the algorithms. Wall-clock time also reflects how well each workload uses the hardware, so it does not establish that ENA is computationally cheaper in general.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Two benchmark families.} Both use related physics changes, with a single morphology in each. Visual, partially observable or structurally different tasks are untested.
\item \textit{Fixed task order.} Each domain uses one training sequence (Mars--Jupiter--Earth; Earth--Heavy-Rough--Mars-Slippery). Ordering effects are untested.
\item \textit{No recurrence during training.} Earlier environments return only at the frozen test. Repeated switches, short dwell times and recall during training are untested.
\item \textit{Test-time choice in the ablation.} Without a Hall of Fame, there are no gladiators to choose from. In that case, ENA-NoSkill and ENA-Plasticity play a random member of their final population in every test episode. The variants with an archive choose among gladiators by Bayesian policy reuse. Adding the Hall of Fame thus changes both what is kept and how the test policy is chosen, and the ablation does not separate the two.
\item \textit{Interaction in the ablation.} The ablation fixes the number of episodes, not of environment steps. A variant with Plasticity takes many times more steps than one without (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The ablation shows what failure-driven search achieves with that interaction. It does not show that allocating effort by failure beats spreading the same effort evenly. Full search was run in a single replicate.
\item \textit{No ablation on HalfCheetah.} Only ENA-Combined ran there. Its retention shows that the full agent kept what it learned, not which skill made it do so.
\item \textit{Archive growth.} The Hall of Fame is capped per niche, and its share of the population is capped, but the number of niches is not. Memory, and the selection's belief over niches, grow with the number of distinct environments. Scaling beyond the three to five environments tested is unknown.
\item \textit{Close physics share a niche.} Physics as close as Earth's and Neptune's share a niche in every replicate.
\item \textit{Detector design chosen on the training regimes.} The chirp's excitation and features were selected on the HalfCheetah training environments. Its confirmation seeds are fresh, but they come from the same three regimes. Generalization to other physics or morphologies is untested.
\item \textit{Resets.} The chirp requires resetting the environment at episode boundaries, to play its \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps}-step excitation from nominal initial states. Candidate evaluation needs more than a reset: a resettable copy of the current physics, built from the environment's own parameters. Both are natural in simulation, digital twins and automated test rigs. They constrain continuous, non-resettable physical systems.
\item \textit{Retrieval evidence not separated.} Bayesian policy reuse outperforms detector-only retrieval. The ablation does not tell how much of that comes from the dynamics likelihoods in its belief, and how much from the test returns.
\end{itemize}
