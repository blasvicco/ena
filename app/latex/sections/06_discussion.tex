\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Answers to the Research Questions}

\textit{RQ1: do the skills mitigate forgetting? Supported, on the tested sequences.} With its skills, ENA recalls the CartPole environment it left behind mid-training, which it loses without them, and its overall test reliability rises substantially (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). The single-policy learners lose most of what they had reached on the environments left behind, in both domains, while ENA keeps it (Sections~\ref{sec:forgetting_cartpole} and~\ref{sec:cheetah}). These findings concern the evaluated implementations, task sequences and budgets.

\textit{RQ1a: is an archive enough by itself? No.} Without detection, the Hall of Fame does not raise reliability, with or without Plasticity, and on ENA-NoSkill it lowers it; organized by detected physics, it brings a resolved gain in both cases (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). Storing proven specialists is not what makes a memory; knowing which physics each was proven in is. How the archive is recalled matters as well: Bayesian policy reuse is more reliable than deploying the recognized niche's highest-Trust gladiator, while whether the niche helps such simple retrieval is not resolved (Section~\ref{sec:selection_ablation}).

\textit{RQ1b: does the memory cost extra interaction? Partly answered.} Archiving and detecting add no measurable candidate evaluation of their own; their cost is bookkeeping and detection time (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). With Plasticity, however, adding the Hall of Fame comes with more candidate steps, an effect our data do not resolve. The archive is filled at no cost, but it may change how much the failure-driven search spends.

\textit{RQ1c: can the memory be built online, without labels? Yes in both domains, with different drivers.} On CartPole the transition-model detector separates the training environments, with the fragmentation, false alarms and merged physics of Table~\ref{tab:detection}. On HalfCheetah the same kind of detector fails, because a learning population's changing behavior reaches contact states its models have not seen; the chirp, whose response does not depend on the agent, meets the detection target (Section~\ref{sec:detection_benchmark}). Neither driver uses a gradient. The chirp, however, was selected and confirmed on the same three physical regimes it is used on.

\textit{RQ2: does failure-driven search pay? Supported.} Plasticity is the largest single contribution of the ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), so searching in proportion to failure is what produces specialists worth archiving. Searching at full effort in every episode costs an order of magnitude more candidate evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:compute}); that cost is set by construction, since full effort evaluates every other specialist for the maximum number of steps in every generation. The quadratic controller was not worse than the ramp and may be better (Section~\ref{sec:controller}), so the finding concerns the principle of scaling search to failure, not the linear shape we use.

\subsection{Why the Archive Needs Detection}
We read RQ1a's result as a problem of recall, not of storage. Without niches, the archive is one pool of gladiators proven in different physics: they are re-injected into the population regardless of the current environment and take places that evolution would otherwise use, and at test the selection has no physical evidence with which to tell a Jupiter specialist from an Earth one. With detection, every gladiator carries the physics it was proven in, the dynamics likelihoods of the selection~\eqref{eq:bpr} point the selection to the matching niche, and the specialists of one environment can no longer be evicted by another's. The full combination also makes the outcome much less dependent on the initial population, as the spread of its reliability across replicates shows (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

Recall depends on more than the niche. Bayesian policy reuse differs from niche match in two ways: it weighs each episode's dynamics likelihoods into a belief instead of following the detector's single decision, and it learns from the test returns. The selection ablation does not separate the two. The returns at least let it abandon a gladiator that fails in its own niche, which niche match cannot do; over a test, niche match tries far fewer individuals (Section~\ref{sec:selection_ablation}). A gladiator archived for solving a niche can still fail there at test, and the archive holds several per niche; without evidence from the returns, a failing one keeps playing.

\subsection{Retention, Adaptation and Absolute Return}
\label{sec:retention}
ENA's memory has a price, and the price differs by domain. On CartPole, ENA solves a new environment later than PPO, because its search spends steps evaluating many candidates before one is good enough, and SAC-Discrete scores slightly higher on the environments it does not forget; ENA trades a few points there for keeping the one environment the single-policy learners lose. On HalfCheetah the trade-off is sharper: ENA keeps what it learned, but it learns far less than SAC within the same budget, since candidate evaluation and the chirp leave it far fewer learning episodes per environment than SAC (Section~\ref{sec:forgetting_protocol}). What SAC loses on the environments left behind exceeds the return ENA reached there at the end of each phase (Section~\ref{sec:cheetah}). ENA's retention above 100\% has a mundane cause: end-of-phase training returns average over active evolutionary search, exploratory mutants and candidates included, whereas the test deploys a recalled, Olympic-confirmed specialist frozen and without exploration noise.

A specialist archive therefore pays off when environments recur and relearning them is expensive relative to the cost of the search that found them. When an environment is unlikely to return, or when the budget per environment is large enough for a gradient learner to relearn it quickly, a single strong policy that is partly forgotten may be worth more than a weaker one kept intact.

\subsection{Scope of the Comparison}
\label{sec:scope}
The single-policy learners measure how severe forgetting is when a policy optimizer has no architectural memory; the claim of this paper rests on the skill ablation. Two asymmetries follow. First, at test, every agent's weights are frozen, but ENA still routes among its archived specialists with an updating belief, whereas the single-policy learners deploy one policy; the comparison contrasts context-indexed specialist preservation with single-policy learning, not two equally equipped systems. Second, the continual-RL methods closest to ENA are not baselines here, for reasons that differ between them. OWL~\cite{kessler2022same} and TeLAPA~\cite{lillo2026beyond} are told where tasks begin and end: OWL trains the head of the task it is given and infers the task only at test, and TeLAPA builds a task's archive, and retrieves a starting point from its archives, at each task boundary it is given. Both build their memory during the stream, but from labelled tasks. Run on our streams, they would need either the task boundaries, which no agent here receives, or a detector to supply them, which would amount to adding ENA's environment-detection skill to them. ITE~\cite{cully2015robots} builds its archive offline, before deployment, and adapts once to each damage, starting when performance drops below a user-set threshold; it neither keeps a memory across a sequence of conditions nor recognizes one that returns. RL-CD~\cite{dasilva2006dealing}, MBCD~\cite{alegre2021minimum} and SWOKS~\cite{dick2024statistical} do detect contexts online without labels and keep a policy per context, so they address the same problem. A comparison with them, however, would be confounded: RL-CD's models are tabular and need a discretized state space, while MBCD and SWOKS learn by gradient descent, so a difference could come from the learning algorithm rather than from the memory. We therefore leave it to the hybrid ENA of Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}, which can share their learner; a gradient-trained policy per niche, with ENA's own detector and selection, would also show whether the skills mitigate forgetting beyond ENA's evolutionary loop. Finally, the wall-clock times are measured with our own implementations on one machine; environment steps and candidate steps are properties of the algorithms, but wall-clock time also reflects how well each workload uses the hardware, so it does not establish that ENA is computationally cheaper in general.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Two benchmark families} with related physics changes, a single morphology in each. Visual, partially observable or structurally different tasks are untested.
\item \textit{Fixed task order.} Each domain uses one training sequence (Mars--Jupiter--Earth; Earth--Heavy-Rough--Mars-Slippery). Ordering effects are untested.
\item \textit{No recurrence during training.} Earlier environments return only at the frozen test. Repeated switches, short dwell times and recall during training are untested.
\item \textit{Archive growth.} The Hall of Fame is capped per niche, and its share of the population is capped, but the number of niches is not. Memory, and the selection's belief over niches, grow with the number of distinct environments; scaling beyond the three to five environments tested is unknown.
\item \textit{Linear transition models.} Physics a linear model cannot tell apart (such as Earth and Neptune) share a niche.
\item \textit{Detector design chosen on the training regimes.} The chirp's excitation and features were selected on the HalfCheetah training environments; its confirmation seeds are fresh, but come from the same three regimes. Generalization to other physics or morphologies is untested.
\item \textit{Resets.} The chirp requires resetting the environment at episode boundaries to play its \Res{cfg/cheetah/chirp/steps}-step excitation from nominal initial states, and candidate evaluation requires running episodes in fresh copies of the environment. Both are natural in simulation, digital twins and automated test rigs, but constrain continuous, non-resettable physical systems.
\item \textit{Retrieval evidence not separated.} Bayesian policy reuse outperforms detector-only retrieval, but the ablation does not tell how much of that comes from the dynamics likelihoods in its belief and how much from the test returns.
\end{itemize}
