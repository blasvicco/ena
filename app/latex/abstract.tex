Reinforcement learning agents trained with gradients tend to forget an environment once they adapt to a new one. We present the Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA), a gradient-free agent for non-stationary environments that keeps a memory of specialist policies organized by the physics it detects. ENA evolves a population of small neural networks, scaling its search effort to how badly the current specialist fails, and archives proven specialists in a Hall of Fame. Per-environment linear-Gaussian dynamics models and a sequential change detector (the Predictive-Mixture CuSum) notice when the physics change and recognize physics seen before, and Bayesian policy reuse chooses which archived specialist to deploy, combining the evidence of the dynamics models with the observed returns. A skill ablation over \Res{ablation/n} replicates on a non-stationary CartPole shows that the skills are complementary: together they raise test reliability from \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\%. The failure-driven search contributes most, and the archive helps only when environment detection organizes it.
