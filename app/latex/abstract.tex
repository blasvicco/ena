Agents that keep learning in a changing environment tend to forget earlier environments once they adapt to new ones. We ask whether a few skills can turn the individuals of an evolving population into a memory that mitigates this catastrophic forgetting. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) evolves small neural networks without gradients. It searches harder when its current specialist fails. It archives the specialists that prove themselves, sorted by the physics it detects on its own, and recalls them by Bayesian policy reuse. In a skill ablation on a non-stationary CartPole, only the variants with failure-driven search learn the harder environments. Among them, only the one that also has an archive organized by environment detection keeps what it learned. As a reference, standard single-policy learners trained on the same streams, on CartPole and HalfCheetah, forget much of what they learned, while ENA keeps it. ENA, however, adapts more slowly than PPO on CartPole, and reaches lower returns than SAC on HalfCheetah. We do not claim that ENA outperforms other continual-learning methods.
