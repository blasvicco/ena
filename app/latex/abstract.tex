Agents that keep learning in a non-stationary environment tend to forget an earlier environment once they adapt to a new one. We ask whether a small set of skills can turn the individuals an evolving population produces into a memory that mitigates this catastrophic forgetting. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) evolves small neural networks without gradients, scales its search effort to how badly the current specialist fails, archives the networks that prove themselves by the physics it detects without being told, and recalls them by Bayesian policy reuse. A skill ablation over \Res{ablation/n} replicates on a non-stationary CartPole is the main evidence: together, the skills raise test reliability, the share of test episodes that reach the success threshold, from \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\%. Failure-driven search, which spends more interaction, is what lets ENA learn the harder environments at all; among the variants that learn them, only the archive organized by environment detection keeps them, holding \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% of the return on the environment left behind mid-sequence against \Res{ablation/ENA-Plasticity/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% with failure-driven search alone; and Bayesian reuse recalls from the archive better than the detector alone. As a reference, single-policy learners (DQN, PPO and SAC-Discrete) trained on the same CartPole stream keep at most \Res{challenge/DQN/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% of their return on that environment, against \Res{challenge/ENA-Combined/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% for ENA; on HalfCheetah, PPO and SAC lose \Res{cheetah/PPO/forgetting/left/mean} and \Res{cheetah/SAC/forgetting/left/mean} of their end-of-phase return, on average over the two environments left behind, and ENA none. PPO adapts faster on CartPole, and SAC reaches higher returns on HalfCheetah. We do not claim that ENA outperforms other continual-learning methods.
