Agents that keep learning in a non-stationary environment tend to forget an earlier environment once they adapt to a new one. We ask whether a small set of skills can turn the individuals an evolving population produces into a memory that mitigates this catastrophic forgetting. The Evolutionary Neuroplastic Agent (ENA) evolves small neural networks without gradients, scales its search effort to how badly the current specialist fails, archives the networks that prove themselves by the physics it detects without being told, and recalls them by Bayesian policy reuse. A skill ablation over \Res{ablation/n} replicates on a non-stationary CartPole is the main evidence: together, the skills raise test reliability, the share of test episodes that reach the success threshold, from \Res{ablation/ENA-NoSkill/test/rel/mean}\% to \Res{ablation/ENA-Combined/test/rel/mean}\%. Failure-driven search contributes most; the archive helps only when environment detection organizes it, and Bayesian reuse recalls from it better than the detector alone. As a reference, single-policy learners (DQN, PPO, SAC) trained on the same streams keep at most \Res{challenge/DQN/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% of their return on a CartPole environment left behind, against \Res{challenge/ENA-Combined/retention/Jupiter-Heavy/mean}\% for ENA; on HalfCheetah, PPO and SAC lose \Res{cheetah/PPO/forgetting/left/mean} and \Res{cheetah/SAC/forgetting/left/mean} of it, and ENA none. SAC, however, reaches higher absolute returns, and PPO adapts faster. We do not claim that ENA outperforms other continual-learning methods.
